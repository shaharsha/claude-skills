# Model Selection & Cost (cross-provider)

> **Scope - read this when *choosing* a model, not when *prompting* one.** Which tier for a task, at what budget, good at what. For *how to write the prompt* once you've picked, use `claude.md` / `gpt.md` / `gemini.md` / `kimi.md` / `grok.md`. Keep this file **out of the prompt-authoring context** - benchmark tables are low-signal noise while writing a prompt (the "context rot" caution in SKILL.md Section A applies to the skill itself).
>
> **Staleness - the numbers are a dated snapshot, the *relationships* are the durable part.** Sources: Artificial Analysis (Intelligence Index **v5**, pulled **2026-11-07**; Claude Fable 5.2 rows **2026-11-10**; Gemini 4 rows **2026-11-13**; other models' rows unchanged on re-check), Arena (formerly LMArena) Agent arena (**2026-11-11**), SemiAnalysis GDPx (**v1.0**, board read **2026-11-07**), BullshitBench (v2 data through **2026-10-13**; no newer runs as of 2026-11-13). **AA moved to Intelligence Index v5 on 2026-10-27, and v5 scores are not comparable to v4.3** (or to the July or September snapshots) - v5 swapped most of the evals, so every model scores differently; never compare a v5 number with an older one. AA's live numbers also drift after launch (Opus 5.5 `max` was 58 in the v5 announcement and reads 59.1 now; Carbon launched at 59 and reads 60.0). Releases land weekly; trust the **bands, gaps, and shape of the curves**, and re-pull before relying on a figure (see *Refreshing the AA data* at the end).

## Intelligence, cost, and speed - per model and effort (AA Intelligence Index v5, pulled 2026-11-07; Fable 5.2 2026-11-10; Gemini 4 2026-11-13)

Every effort level AA tests for the current models. **Intel** = AA Intelligence Index v5. **Cost / task** = what AA actually paid per Intelligence Index task (list prices × tokens really used, incl. reasoning and cache reads/writes) - the number to compare, not $/1M. **AA now costs Gemini 4 Carbon and Argon at the $2 / $10 intro price** (Nov 13 pull; on Nov 7 it used the $4 / $20 list price - Carbon $6.59-7.76, Argon $9.95 per task). Their price doubles on Dec 1; at the same token use that doubles these costs (Carbon $9.36-10.96, Argon $9.94 per task - Argon's doubled figure is within a cent of its Nov 7 list-price cost ($9.95), while Carbon's Nov 7 costs came from an earlier run). Gemini 3.8 Flash is at its $0.75 / $3.75 intro price. **Time / task** = AA's *estimated* generation time per task: "output tokens per task divided by output speed, weighted by each benchmark's weight in the Intelligence Index" - it leaves out tool execution, queueing, and time to first token, so a full agent run takes longer (v5 tasks are much longer and more agentic than v4.3's). **tok/s** = median output speed. **Halluc.** = AA-Omniscience v1.1 hallucination rate (answers wrong instead of abstaining on knowledge it lacks; lower is better - Haiku and Flash-Lite score low partly by abstaining, not by knowing more). "-" = not published. Ordered by each model's best score. Superseded models and models without a full set of v5 rows are omitted (GPT-6 Sol, GPT-5.6 Terra, Claude Fable 5.1 - best v5 row `xhigh` 50.0 at $10.85 per task - Kimi K3, and Gemini 3.1 Pro; `gpt.md` quotes 5.6 Terra's v5 `max` row for comparison).

### What the curves say

- **Cost frontier (most intelligence per dollar), at Gemini's intro price: GPT-6 Luna → GPT-6.1 Terra → GPT-6.1 Sol → GPT-6 Astra → Claude Fable 5.2 `medium` → Gemini 4 Carbon → Claude Fable 5.2 `high` and up.** Luna `medium` covers ~19 points for $0.04 (Claude Haiku 5.5 `low` sits marginally on it); **Terra covers 29-42 for $0.07-0.26** (`low`/`medium`/`high`); **6.1 Sol covers 35-55 for $0.16-1.27**; then Astra `high`/`xhigh` (54.8-55.3, $3.24-3.66), **Fable 5.2 `medium` (56.4, $3.80)**, Carbon `medium`/`high` (58.3-60.0, $4.68-5.48), and **Fable 5.2 `high`/`xhigh`/`max` (61.8-64.6, $9.08-26.54)** at the top. **Opus 5.5 is off it for now:** Carbon `medium` beats Opus 5.5 `xhigh` (57.4, $6.05) and Carbon `high` beats Opus 5.5 `max` (59.1, $10.04) on both counts - but only until Carbon's price doubles on Dec 1: at $4 / $20 its costs double (`medium` $9.36, `high` $10.96), Fable 5.2 `high` (61.8, $9.08) then beats both, Carbon drops off the frontier, and Opus 5.5 `xhigh` is back on it. Fable 5.2 `low` (53.9, $3.10) is just off it (6.1 Sol `max` scores 54.6 for $1.27), but it beats Opus 5.5 `high` (51.8, $3.46) on both score and cost. **Gemini 4 Hydrogen is off it at every level** (6.1 Sol scores more for less: `xhigh` 52.5 for $0.76 vs Hydrogen `high` 50.8 for $1.87).
- **GPT-6.1 Sol is the value leader by a wide margin:** `max` (54.6, $1.27) ties Sonnet 5.5 `max` (54.5, $10.02) at an eighth of the cost and sits within ~5 points of Opus 5.5 `max` and Carbon `high` for an eighth and a quarter of their cost per task (Fable 5.2 `max` scores 10 points more at ~21× the cost); `high` (50.3, $0.59) matches Argon `high` (50.4, $4.97) and Hydrogen `high` (50.8, $1.87).
- **Effort matters more in v5 than it did in v4.3.** Opus 5.5 spans 35 → 59 from `low` to `max` at 10× the cost, Sonnet 5.5 26 → 55, 6.1 Sol 35 → 55, Astra 43 → 57. Fable 5.2 spans 54 → 65 and gets most of it by its default `high` (61.8; `max` adds 2.8 points for ~3× the cost). The flat curves are Carbon (54 → 60), Grok 4.7 (31 → 36), and Fable 5.1, whose `xhigh` (50.0) beat its `max` (49.2). So the old "sweep effort first" rule now has a larger payoff, and a model's default effort can understate it badly (Opus 5.5 `medium` is 48.2, Sonnet 5.5 `high` is 41.5).
- **Claude Sonnet 5.5 is cheap per token, not per task, at high effort.** `max` costs about what Opus 5.5 `max` costs ($10.02 vs $10.04) for 4.6 fewer points, and `high` (41.5, $1.64) costs nearly 3× GPT-6.1 Sol `high` (50.3, $0.59). Its case is speed (97-135 tok/s), Claude-specific behavior, and ZDR; if you need it at `xhigh`+, price Opus 5.5 `high`/`xhigh` first.
- **Claude Fable 5.2 is the new #1 and resets the Claude top end.** `high` (61.8, $9.08, ~98K output tokens per task) beats every other model's best score for less than Opus 5.5 `max`; `max` (64.6, $26.54, ~274K output tokens) is the most expensive run in the table. It hallucinates like the other big Claude and GPT models (56-60%), its estimated generation times are long at the top (1,059-2,784 s), and it needs 30-day retention by default. Its predecessor **Fable 5.1 is superseded on AA** (best `xhigh` 50.0 at $10.85, below Argon and Terra `max`).
- **Gemini is strong but token-hungry per task.** Argon `high` uses ~116K output tokens per task (2.2× Astra `max`) and costs $4.97 at the intro price ($9.95 at list); Carbon uses ~123K (29% fewer than Opus 5.5 `max`). **Carbon's `low` is not cheaper than its `medium`** (54.2 at $5.22 vs 58.3 at $4.68) and scores lower. **Gemini 4 Hydrogen is among the fastest per token** (237-256 tok/s; only Flash-Lite and Haiku 5.5 are in that range) and estimated generation times are short (474 s at its default `high`), but per task it costs more than GPT-6.1 Sol at the same score; its `high` (50.8) edges Argon (50.4) for less than half the cost. **Gemini 3.8 Flash is fast but not cheap per task** (30.8 for $7.77 even at intro prices - Hydrogen `medium` scores 41.2 for $0.80, GPT-6.1 Terra `low` 29.4 for $0.07).
- **Time frontier (shortest estimated generation time per level):** Terra and 6.1 Sol at `low`/`medium` plus Astra `low` (64-246 s) for 29-46; then **Fable 5.2 from `low` up** - 388 s for 53.9, 501 s for 56.4, 1,059 s for 61.8 - with only Opus 5.5 `xhigh` (953 s, 57.4) between; Fable 5.2 is fast per token (75-95 tok/s), so above ~52 points it needs less time than Astra (391-630 s at 52-55), Hydrogen `xhigh` (819 s for 53.9), or Carbon `medium` (1,451 s). Hydrogen is fast per token but not on this frontier: Astra `low` and `medium` reach the same scores in similar or less time. Kimi K3.1 Pro is the slowest per token (27 tok/s, 2,169 s per task; only Fable 5.2 `max` takes longer per task). These are generation-time estimates; tool calls and queueing add to them.
- **Hallucination varies by model and effort, not just intelligence.** Gemini dominates the low end: **Argon 4%, Carbon 4% at `medium` and 11% at `high`** (20% at `low`), **Hydrogen 8-12% from `medium` up** (19% at `low`), then Grok 4.7 (20%), Haiku 4.5 (23%), Flash-Lite (27%), Haiku 5.5 (34-40%), Terra (35-42%), and Kimi K3.1 Pro (37%). The big Claude and GPT models answer instead of abstaining far more often (Astra 43-50%, 6.1 Sol 48-52%, Fable 5.2 56-60%, Opus 5.5 50-67%), and Luna guesses most (79-85%) - pair those with retrieval and an explicit "say you don't know" instruction.

AA v5 sub-benchmarks where published (Nov 7 pull unless noted): **FrontierCode 2.0** (Cognition's maintainer-merge coding test) - Opus 5.5 `max` 46% leads, then Astra `max`, Terra `max`, and Sonnet 5.5 `max` at 42%, Carbon 41%; **Terminal-Bench 4.0** - Kimi K3.1 Pro 67%, Carbon 65%, Sonnet 5.5 `max` 64%; **Terminal-Bench-Science** - Carbon 65%, Astra `max` 63%; **HLE-Diamond** - Carbon 69%, Opus 5.5 `max` 68%; **CritPt** (hard subset) - Astra 72%, Carbon 69%; **AA-Briefcase** (agentic knowledge work, launch figures) - Sonnet 5.5 led at 1,812 Elo, Carbon 1,609; **AA-Omniscience v1.1** - Carbon 54, Opus 5.5 `max` 48, Argon 46.

## What each is good at

- **Claude Fable 5.2** (Nov 9) - **AA v5 #1** (64.6 at `max`; 61.8 at its default `high` for $9.08, above every other model's best). Anthropic reports it ahead of Opus 5.5 on nearly every benchmark it ran (FrontierCode 2.0 49.1% vs 45.5% at `xhigh`, Terminal-Bench-Science 74.6% vs 58.7%, Remote Labor Index 41.3% vs 21.3%; Opus 5.5 keeps the higher AA-Briefcase Elo). **#1 on the Agent arena** (Nov 11, max: 30.5% net improvement, a clear lead); not yet on GDPx or BullshitBench. $8/$40 (cache reads 0.025×), **30-day retention by default** (ZDR only by Anthropic's authorization; AWS offers it to eligible Bedrock customers for internal use through at least June 30 2027), long turns at `high`+. Anthropic still says start on Opus 5.5 and move up when your evals at higher effort fall short.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon** (**GA Nov 10**, paid tier; `gemini-4-carbon`) - **AA v5 #2** (60.0 at `high`; `medium`, the default, 58.3 for $4.68 at the intro price), with the lowest hallucination rates among top scorers (4% at `medium`) and led HLE-Diamond and Terminal-Bench-Science on AA's Nov 7 pull (Anthropic reports Fable 5.2 at 74.6% on Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1); **#3 on Arena's Agent arena** (Nov 11, preliminary) and **#2 on GDPx** (10.9% at $16.29 per task, a third of Opus 5.5's cost). Google claims ~2× fewer unsupported claims than Argon and 0.0% prompt-injection success on Gray Swan's benchmark. $2 / $10 through Nov 30, then $4 / $20 - price it at $4 / $20 for anything that runs past November. Slow, long turns (1,451-1,702 s estimated per task).
- **Claude Opus 5.5** - Anthropic's recommended default and the **strongest model with standard ZDR eligibility** (Fable 5.2 gets ZDR only by authorization or AWS's eligible-customer offer): AA v5 #3 (59.1 at `max`), **#2 on the Agent arena** (Nov 11, behind Fable 5.2) and **#1 on GDPx** (12.0%; Fable 5.2 not yet on it), and the FrontierCode 2.0 leader on AA's Nov 7 pull. Anthropic reports Fable-5.1-level work at ~40% lower cost than Opus 5, stronger long-running coding, knowledge work with fewer invented figures, and the lowest prompt-injection rate it has measured (tied with Fable 5.1). ## Arena (formerly LMArena) - pick the arena that matches your task

Arena runs a **separate human-vote leaderboard per task** (Agent, Text/chat, WebDev/Code, Vision, Search, Document, image/video). Use the task-matched one - the general Text/chat arena scatters agentic models because chat preference ≠ agentic capability. **Agent arena, Nov 11 2026** (2.93M sessions, 64 models; "net improvement"): **#1 Claude Fable 5.2 (max) 30.5%**, alone in its rank range; #2 Claude Opus 5.5 (max) 19.5%; #3 Gemini 4 Carbon (high, preliminary) 16.9%; #4 Claude Sonnet 5.5 (max); #5 Gemini 4 Hydrogen (high, preliminary); #6 Fable 5.1 (max); #7 GPT-6 Astra (max); #8 Opus 5.5 (high); #9 GPT-6.1 Sol (max); #10 GPT-6.1 Terra (max); #11 Gemini 4 Argon (high). Ranks 2-10 overlap in their confidence ranges. On Nov 8, before Fable 5.2 and Hydrogen were listed: #1 Opus 5.5, #2 Carbon, #3 Sonnet 5.5, #4 Fable 5.1, #5 Astra, #6 GPT-6.1 Sol, #7 GPT-6.1 Terra, #8 Argon, #13 Kimi K3.1 Pro. The fuller **Nov 4 snapshot** (2.76M sessions, 58 models; "net improvement" score): **#1 Claude Opus 5.5 (max), #2 Gemini 4 Carbon (high), #3 Claude Sonnet 5.5 (max), #4 GPT-6 Astra (max), #5 Fable 5.1 (max), #6 GPT-6.1 Sol (max), #7 GPT-6.1 Terra (max), #8 Gemini 4 Argon (high)**, #14 Kimi K3.1 Pro (preliminary), #17 Grok 4.7, #18 Gemini 3.8 Flash, #23 GPT-6 Luna, #28 Haiku 5.5 (high). Ranks 2-7 have overlapping confidence ranges, so treat them as one band. Per-signal leaders: confirmed success and praise vs. complaint → Opus 5.5; landing user corrections → Carbon. (Earlier snapshots, for history: Sept 15 had Fable 5.1 #1 and Astra #2, before Opus 5.5 shipped; Oct 8 had Opus 5.5 #1, Astra #2, Fable 5.1 #3, and GPT-6.1 Sol least likely to hallucinate tools it doesn't have, 0.51%.) Google also reports Carbon at #4 on WebDev Arena (1768, +89 over Argon).

**⚠️ On Opus 4.8, thinking is load-bearing for tool use** (July Agent-arena finding): with adaptive thinking ON it had ~0.2% tool hallucination, with thinking OFF (its API default) ~19%. Set `thinking:{type:"adaptive"}` for any tool-using agent on Opus 4.8. Every newer Claude model has thinking on by default or always on.

**Bottom line:** among the models all three have scored, AA (objective evals), the task-matched arena, and GDPx (below) agree on the top band - Opus 5.5, Gemini 4 Carbon, GPT-6 Astra - and disagree mainly on Fable 5.1, which still ranks well with human voters but is weak per dollar on AA v5. **Fable 5.2 now leads both AA (by ~5 points) and the Agent arena (by ~11)**; GDPx hasn't scored it yet. Weight **AA for test-graded capability/cost**, **Arena for which output humans prefer**, and **GDPx for long professional-domain agent work**.

## Beyond coding - professional-domain agent work (SemiAnalysis GDPx)

[GDPx](https://gdpx.semianalysis.com/) (v1.0, Nov 2 2026) is the third reference, for **long agentic work in professional domains other than coding**: 206 tasks across 11 domains (RSI, chip design, cybersecurity, biology, finance, legal, quant trading, hardware design, materials science, alignment, software engineering) from 14 specialist data partners, run in live environments (real repos, a replica PE deal room, an EHR, lab-instrument simulators) with up to **8 hours per task**, in each model's native harness (Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity; OpenCode otherwise). Domains are weighted equally (and partners within a domain), grading is binary or continuous depending on the domain, the default score averages 3 attempts, and $/task is published. It is a true frontier benchmark - the best model scores 12%.

**Overall board, Nov 7 2026** (score, $/task): **Claude Opus 5.5 12.0% ($51.41)**, **Gemini 4 Carbon 10.9% ($16.29)**, GPT-6 Astra 9.6% ($53.99), Muse Wonder Full 9.0% (no price), Claude Fable 5.1 8.5% ($67.16), GPT-6.1 Sol 8.4% ($9.80), Claude Sonnet 5.5 8.2% ($45.27), Gemini 4 Argon 7.3% ($12.79), Muse Wonder 6.8%, DeepSeek 4.1 Flash 5.4% ($0.94), GPT-6 Luna 5.1% ($0.92), Grok 4.7 3.9% ($16.34), GLM 5.3 2.7%. Things to know before using it:

- **Safety classifiers move the ranking.** SemiAnalysis says Opus 5.5 and Astra are "sandbagged" by their classifiers in cyber, biology, and alignment; the site's **"Exclude domains affected by safety classifiers"** board (8 domains) reorders the top: **Astra 11.8%, Opus 5.5 11.7%, GPT-6.1 Sol 10.6%, Sonnet 5.5 9.7%, Carbon 9.2%**, Fable 5.1 8.6%. Carbon's overall #2 leans on biology, which SemiAnalysis attributes "likely" to weaker safety classifiers. Pick the board that matches whether your domain trips classifiers.
- **Finance and legal are effectively unsolved.** Under the intended (binary) grading every model scores 0% on legal, and on finance only Carbon scores above zero (2.8%); the site's "Partial score" metric shows the continuous credit.
- **Domains are spiky.** Per SemiAnalysis, Opus 5.5 wins quant trading, hardware design, and chip design; Astra wins RSI and materials science; Carbon wins biology and edges software engineering. **On the software-engineering domain (Datacurve, 15 tasks) Claude Sonnet 5.5 ties Carbon at 11.1% and ranks above Opus 5.5 (8.9%, tied with GPT-6.1 Sol and Argon)** - with 15 tasks × 3 attempts that is about one solved attempt, so read it as "Sonnet 5.5 at `max` is not behind Opus 5.5 on this kind of work," not as a lead.
- **Caveats:** a commercial SemiAnalysis product built with data vendors that sell to the labs, its domains picked to match SemiAnalysis's own AI-revenue demand model; mostly private partner tasks (one public task per partner); not independently replicated; one evaluator's harness choices. Directional, like the others.

## Beyond intelligence - false-premise detection (BullshitBench)

A capability AA, Arena, and GDPx miss: **does the model call out a nonsensical or false premise instead of building on it?** On [BullshitBench](https://petergpt.github.io/bullshit-benchmark/) (v2: 100 nonsense prompts, 13 techniques, 5 domains, models run with no system prompt; clear-pushback rate over **all attempts** from `leaderboard.csv`, repo pulled 2026-09-23 - the dashboard's headline instead **excludes refusals** from the denominator, so heavy refusers such as Fable 5.x read ~10 points higher there): **Claude Opus 4.8 is still #1 at ~95%**; the Claude 5 generation is lower - **Sonnet 5 ~80%, Fable 5.1 64-77%, Opus 5 70-73%**; **GPT-6 Astra jumped to 64-69%** (GPT-5.6 Sol ~47%, Terra ~47-54%, Luna ~37-41%); **Gemini 3.7 Flash ~31-35%**. The durable lessons hold: **raising effort doesn't help and often hurts** (Fable 5.1: 77% at `low` vs 64% at `max`), and **refusal ≠ pushback** (Fable 5.x declines 11-36% of prompts instead of naming the flaw). **v2 additions through Oct 13 (clear-pushback rate):** Opus 5.5 62-63%, Sonnet 5.5 58-60%, **Haiku 5.5 53-57%** (Haiku 4.5: 77%), GPT-6.1 Sol 59-65%, GPT-6 Sol 54-59%, GPT-6 Luna 58-59%, **Gemini 4 Argon 65-66%** (but 13-15% outright acceptance vs ~4% for Opus 5.5 / Astra), Kimi K3 44-58%, Grok 4.6 56-66% - and again **higher effort scored lower** for Argon, Kimi K3, and Grok 4.3/4.6. The whole Claude 5.5 family pushes back less than Opus 4.8 / Haiku 4.5 did. Not yet benchmarked (no new runs between Oct 13 and Nov 13): Claude Fable 5.2, Gemini 4 Carbon, Gemini 4 Hydrogen, GPT-6.1 Terra, Kimi K3.1 Pro, Grok 4.7, Gemini 3.8 Flash. If your app must not act on broken user premises, weight this - and fix it with explicit prompting + model choice, not the effort knob.

## Picking a model - decision rules

- **Highest ceiling** → **Claude Fable 5.2** (AA v5 #1; its default `high` scores 61.8 for $9.08, less than Opus 5.5 `max`) when 30-day retention is acceptable (or you have Anthropic's ZDR authorization, or qualify for AWS's ZDR offer on Bedrock) and turns can run long - Anthropic still recommends starting on Opus 5.5 and moving up when your evals at higher effort fall short; **Claude Opus 5.5** (AA v5 #3 at `max`, #2 on the Agent arena and #1 on GDPx, $4/$20, ZDR) as the default top pick and the strongest option under standard ZDR; **Gemini 4 Carbon** (GA Nov 10) is AA v5 #2, #3 on the Agent arena and #2 on GDPx at about a third of Opus's GDPx cost per task - price it at the $4 / $20 it costs from Dec 1, and expect long turns; **GPT-6 Astra** when you want OpenAI's stack, computer use, or template-faithful documents (and #1 on GDPx outside the safety-classifier domains); **Gemini 4 Argon** when you want Google's stack, the lowest hallucination rate, or long-context/video work (price doubles Dec 1); **Kimi K3.1 Pro** when you want open weights (released Nov 4) at the top of the open-weight band.
- **Claude, Opus 5.5 or Fable 5.2** → price them per task, not per token: on AA v5, Fable 5.2 `low` (53.9, $3.10) beats Opus 5.5 `high` (51.8, $3.46), and Fable 5.2 `medium` (56.4, $3.80) is within a point of Opus 5.5 `xhigh` (57.4, $6.05) for ~37% less. The catches: 30-day retention by default instead of ZDR, a separate rate limit, longer turns, and prompts that need the Fable 5.2 review in `claude-fable.md`.
- **Default agentic coding** → **Opus 5.5** (FrontierCode 2.0 leader on AA's Nov 7 pull; Anthropic reports Fable 5.2 higher), starting at its `medium` default and raising effort for hard tasks (v5 rewards effort: `high` 51.8, `xhigh` 57.4); for the best capability-per-dollar, **GPT-6.1 Sol** (`high`/`xhigh`); on Claude, Sonnet 5.5 at `medium` for well-specified tasks (fast) - at `xhigh`+ Opus 5.5 is usually cheaper per task, though Sonnet 5.5 at `max` matched the top on GDPx's software-engineering domain.
- **Everyday business / support / internal tools** → **GPT-6.1 Sol** (`low`/`medium`) or **GPT-6.1 Terra** (`medium`/`high`), or **Sonnet 5.5** (`low`/`medium`) when you need Claude behavior.
- **Fast multimodal / agentic work at scale** → **Gemini 4 Hydrogen** (`medium` or `high`; it replaces 3.8 Flash) when throughput and per-token speed are the constraint, alone or as the execution model under Carbon or Argon; if cost per task is the constraint, GPT-6.1 Sol or Terra costs less for the same AA score.
- **High-volume classification / routing / extraction** → **GPT-6 Luna** (or OpenAI's **Decisions API** for typed answers with free output tokens), **Gemini Decision 1** (Google's Decisions API, Nov 11: calibrated yes/no, choice and score answers, zero output tokens, $0.10 per 1M input - `gemini.md`), or **Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite**; on Claude, **Haiku 5.5** at `low`/`medium` (keep prompts under 100K tokens).
- **Mid tier on OpenAI** → **GPT-6.1 Terra** (on the AA cost frontier from 29 to 42 points) for computer use, concise writing, and scoped fixes; GPT-6.1 Sol for harder coding and agentic work.
- **Live X/Twitter data** → Grok 4.7 with server-side `x_search`.
- **Strict instruction-following / adherence** → Claude 4.7+ (literal following) or GPT-6. (Gemini 3.1 Pro led IFBench on AA's old index, which v5 no longer reports; Google itself points Pro-tier work to Argon.)
- **Professional-domain agent work beyond coding** (finance, legal, science, hardware, security) → check the matching GDPx domain board, not the overall score: rankings flip by domain and by whether safety classifiers apply, and finance/legal are unsolved by every model.
- **Must not build on false premises** → Opus 4.8 still leads by far; among current models Fable 5.1 (low) and GPT-6 Astra (max) lead (Fable 5.2 not yet scored), with Gemini 4 Argon and GPT-6.1 Sol close behind; Gemini Flash and Kimi K3 lag. Don't count on the 5.5-generation Claude models here without an explicit premise-check instruction.
- **Data retention constraints** → Fable 5.x requires 30-day retention by default (unless Anthropic authorizes ZDR; for Fable 5.2, AWS also offers eligible Bedrock customers ZDR for internal use through at least June 30 2027); Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra are ZDR-eligible; Gemini 4 models follow the Gemini API's tier terms (paid-tier data isn't used to improve Google's products; Carbon has no free tier); Gemini's Interactions API stores 55 days by default (set `store=false`; new paid `generateContent` projects also store by default since Oct 26); xAI stores Responses 30 days by default (team-wide ZDR disables stateful features); Moonshot offers ZDR to enterprise customers.
- **Before combining models, sweep effort on one.** Anthropic's measurements: a frontier model at *low* effort often beats a cheaper model at its default on cost per solved task, and in every measured case where the work fit one context window and had no cost tail, the lead model alone at lower effort was cheaper than an orchestrator. Two multi-model shapes earn their keep: an **advisor** (cheap executor consults a frontier model on hard decisions - only pays if the executor actually consults, which low effort can suppress) and an **orchestrator** (frontier lead, cheaper workers - pays as insurance against a routine-task cost tail, or when input exceeds one context window).
- **Routing inside an agent loop** → worth it only if the price gap pays for the router. LangChain + NVIDIA Switchyard (Aug 2026): on a 145-task agent suite, escalation routing between a 30B open model and Claude Opus 4.8 sent ~7% of calls to Opus (which still carried 68% of spend), cutting cost 74% for ~6 points of accuracy; the router's judge was 21% of routed spend because it gets no cache benefit. Break-even: **minimum offload share = judge cost ÷ (expensive-model cost − cheap-model cost)** per run - if your two models are close in price this exceeds 100% and routing can't pay (unless the cheap model is self-hosted). Budget for a range, since escalation rates vary run to run; skip routing on latency-critical or short single-turn workloads.
- **Multi-tier pipeline** (the usual pattern): cheap tier for extract-classify-route (Luna / Flash-Lite / Haiku 5.5) → frontier tier for analysis and generation (Opus 5.5 / Sol / Astra / Fable 5.2 / Argon). Google's own recommended split is now Carbon or Argon as orchestrator and verifier with Hydrogen workers (its Antigravity Orchestrator switches between Carbon and Hydrogen automatically); Anthropic's is Opus 5.5 or Sonnet 5.5 with Haiku 5.5 subagents. See SKILL.md Section D.

## Cost-per-task ≠ per-token price

Compare **cost per task at your effort setting**, not headline $/1M. Token efficiency now varies a lot: Opus 5.5 finishes tasks in fewer tokens than Opus 5, GPT-6 Astra uses substantially fewer output tokens than prior GPTs despite a higher per-token price, while Gemini 3.8 Flash deliberately spends *more* tokens (self-verification) - its low per-token price overstates its advantage on long agentic tasks. Cache-read pricing is now a first-order cost on agent loops: 0.025× input on Fable 5.1 / 5.2, 0.05× on Opus 5.5 / Sonnet 5.5 / GPT-6.1 Sol / GPT-6.1 Terra / Gemini 4 Argon, Carbon and Hydrogen, 0.1× on GPT-6 and most others (incl. Haiku 5.5 and Kimi K3.1 Pro), 0.25× on Grok 4.6/4.7 (variants differ - Kimi Highspeed 0.05×, Grok 4.5 0.15×; check the provider file). Long-context price steps change the math too: Haiku 5.5 ×5 above 100K-token prompts, Grok ×2 at ≥200K, GPT-6 ×2 input above 272K. And watch scheduled price changes (Gemini 4 Argon and Carbon double Dec 1 2026; Gemini Flash doubles Jan 1 2027).

## Methodology notes

AA's **Intelligence Index v5** (Oct 27 2026) is built from nine evaluations in four equally weighted pillars: **Knowledge Work** 25% (AA-Briefcase 15%, AutomationBench-AA 5%, GDP.pdf 5%), **Coding** 25% (FrontierCode 2.0 15%, Terminal-Bench 4.0 10%), **Science** 25% (Terminal-Bench-Science 15%, a private hard subset of CritPt v2 10%), and **General** 25% (AA-Omniscience v1.1 15% - 10% accuracy, 5% non-hallucination - and HLE-Diamond 10%). 60% of the index now uses private test sets or held-out answers. New in v5: FrontierCode 2.0 and Terminal-Bench-Science; upgraded: HLE-Diamond (replacing Humanity's Last Exam), AA-Omniscience v1.1, CritPt v2; removed: GDPval-AA, SciCode, and AA-LCR. v5 tasks are far more agentic (median output tokens per task rose from 35K to 59K), which is why effort and token appetite move cost per task so much more than on v4.3. Arena's Agent arena scores "net improvement" from human votes on real agent sessions. GDPx averages pass@1 over 3 attempts per task in native harnesses with up to 8 hours per task. BullshitBench uses a 0/1/2 rubric (accepted nonsense / buried challenge / clear pushback) from a 3-judge panel over 100 prompts × 5 domains. All four are one evaluator's methodology - directional. A model's rank on a public benchmark rarely predicts its rank on *your* task (build your own evals - see `evals.md`).

## Refreshing the AA data

- **The legacy `/api/v2/data/*` endpoints were retired on Nov 4 2026** (they now return 410 Gone). Use **`GET https://artificialanalysis.ai/api/v2/language/models/free`** (header `x-api-key`, same key): it is paginated (`?page=N`, 200 variants per page, `pagination.has_more`; 726 variants on 2026-11-07, 739 on 2026-11-13), reports `intelligence_index_version` (5), and gives per model and effort the intelligence index, `artificial_analysis_intelligence_index_cost.cost_per_task.total_cost`, per-token prices, and a `performance` block with `median_output_tokens_per_second` and time to first answer token. Its cost and intelligence values matched the page data exactly. `/api/v2/language/models` (Pro) has more fields.
- **Time per task and hallucination rate** (and output tokens per task and sub-benchmark scores) are still only in the AA **model pages** (e.g. `artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemini-4-argon`) as Next.js flight data - decode the `self.__next_f.push([1,"…"])` strings and JSON-decode each `{"id":…,"slug":…}` object, keyed by `id` (effort variants share a release slug prefix); fields `name`, `effort.label`, `intelligenceIndex`, `intelligenceIndexCostPerTask.cost.total`, `intelligenceIndexTimePerTask` (output tokens ÷ output speed, benchmark-weighted - an estimate, not measured wall-clock; it uses the page's "Output speed", which can differ from the API's `median_output_tokens_per_second` that the table's tok/s column uses - Fable 5.2 `high` showed 92 on the page vs 76 in the API on Nov 10), `omniscienceHallucinationRate`, `intelligenceIndexOutputTokensPerTask`. **Not every page carries every variant:** on 2026-11-07 some pages (Argon, Kimi K3.1 Pro, Flash-Lite) embedded all 726, others only the ~28 chart defaults - check the count and try another page if it's short. On 2026-11-13 the Carbon and Hydrogen pages carried only their own default variant; each effort has its own page (`/models/gemini-4-hydrogen-high`, `-medium`, `-low`), which carries that variant's metrics. Also check the API's `pricing` fields: AA switched Carbon and Argon from list to intro prices between the Nov 7 and Nov 13 pulls, which moved their cost per task.
