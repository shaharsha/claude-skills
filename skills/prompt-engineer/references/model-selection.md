# Model Selection & Cost (cross-provider)

> **Scope - read this when *choosing* a model, not when *prompting* one.** Which tier for a task, at what budget, good at what. For *how to write the prompt* once you've picked, use `claude.md` / `gpt.md` / `gemini.md` / `kimi.md` / `grok.md`. Keep this file **out of the prompt-authoring context** - benchmark tables are low-signal noise while writing a prompt (the "context rot" caution in SKILL.md Section A applies to the skill itself).
>
> **Staleness - the numbers are a dated snapshot, the *relationships* are the durable part.** Sources: Artificial Analysis (Intelligence Index **v5**; every row re-pulled **2026-11-18**, the day after GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol launched - the other models' scores, costs and hallucination rates had not moved since Nov 17, speeds and times per task had, by up to 6%), Arena (formerly LMArena) Agent arena (**2026-11-11**; the Nov 13 board keeps the same top 11), SemiAnalysis GDPx (**v1.0**, board read **2026-11-18**), BullshitBench (v2 data through **2026-11-18**). **AA moved to Intelligence Index v5 on 2026-10-27, and v5 scores are not comparable to v4.3** (or to the July or September snapshots) - v5 swapped most of the evals, so every model scores differently; never compare a v5 number with an older one. AA's live numbers also drift after launch (Opus 5.5 `max` was 58 in the v5 announcement and reads 59.1 now; Carbon launched at 59 and reads 60.0). **Measured speed drifts more than score or cost:** Carbon `high`'s estimated time per task read 1,702 s on Nov 13, 1,913 s on the afternoon of Nov 17, 2,004 s three hours later and 2,051 s on Nov 18, and Fable 5.2 `high`'s fell from 1,059 s (Nov 10) to 878 s (Nov 17; 892 s on Nov 18), with every score and cost unchanged. **The tok/s column changed basis on 2026-11-17** (AA's Performance v3 speed instead of the older median - see the column notes below), so a tok/s figure here is not comparable with one quoted before that date. Releases land weekly; trust the **bands, gaps, and shape of the curves**, and re-pull before relying on a figure (see *Refreshing the AA data* at the end).

## Intelligence, cost, and speed - per model and effort (AA Intelligence Index v5, pulled 2026-11-18)

Every effort level AA tests for the current models. **Intel** = AA Intelligence Index v5. **Cost / task** = what AA actually paid per Intelligence Index task (list prices × tokens really used, incl. reasoning and cache reads/writes) - the number to compare, not $/1M. **Gemini 4 Carbon and Argon are costed at the $2 / $10 intro price** that Google charges through Nov 30; their token prices double on Dec 1. **For Carbon, AA's API switched to the $4 / $20 list price on Nov 18** (its model pages still use the intro price, which this table keeps): $6.59 per task at `medium`, $7.76 at `high` and $7.19 at `low`, the same figures AA showed on Nov 7. That is less than double; AA's `pricing` fields keep Carbon's cache-write price at $4 under both. Argon has no cache-write price on AA, so at the same token use its costs double ($8.63-9.95 per task; AA read $9.95 at `high` on Nov 7, when it used the list price). Gemini 3.8 Flash is at its $0.75 / $3.75 intro price. **Time / task** = AA's *estimated* generation time per task: "output tokens per task divided by output speed, weighted by each benchmark's weight in the Intelligence Index" - it leaves out tool execution, and it reflects the load on AA's test requests, not yours, so a full agent run takes longer (v5 tasks are much longer and more agentic than v4.3's). **tok/s** = AA's headline output speed under its Performance v3 method (Nov 3 2026; `performance_v3.mean_output_tokens_per_second` in the API): output tokens, reasoning included, per second over each request's full duration - so the wait for the first token counts against it - averaged over 1K-, 10K- and 100K-token prompts and multi-turn agentic tasks, run under concurrent load, across the past 5 days, with requests that fail on the provider's side counted as 0. It is the speed behind Time / task: on Nov 17 output tokens per task ÷ tok/s reproduced AA's time on every row, and on Nov 18 within 1% on 58 of the 59 rows that have a time (GPT-6.2 Astra `medium`, a day after launch, was 8% off: 231 s on its page, 250 s by this formula). **Until 2026-11-17 this column held the older median** (10K-token prompts only; AA stops publishing it on Dec 10 2026). By the new measure the Claude models read 10-49% faster and the Gemini 4 models 12-35% slower than by the old one, so don't mix the two. AA counts tokens as each provider's API reports them, so a token is not the same unit across vendors: compare Time / task across vendors and tok/s within one. **Halluc.** = AA-Omniscience v1.1 hallucination rate (answers wrong instead of abstaining on knowledge it lacks; lower is better - Haiku and Flash-Lite score low partly by abstaining, not by knowing more). "-" = not published. Ordered by each model's best score. OpenAI documents no API default effort for the GPT-6.2 models (it says to start at `medium`), so none is marked. Superseded models and models without a full set of v5 rows are omitted (GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol, which the 6.2 models replaced here on Nov 18 - their last rows are quoted under *Superseded* below - GPT-6 Sol, GPT-5.6 Terra, Claude Fable 5.1 - best v5 row `xhigh` 50.0 at $10.85 per task - Kimi K3, and Gemini 3.1 Pro; `gpt-6.md` quotes 5.6 Terra's v5 `max` row for comparison).

### What the curves say

- **Cost frontier (most intelligence per dollar): GPT-6 Luna → GPT-6.1 Terra → GPT-6.2 Sol → GPT-6.2 Astra → Claude Fable 5.2 `xhigh` and `max`.** OpenAI's models now hold it from 10 to 62 points, apart from two marginal Claude Haiku 5.5 rows. Luna `medium` covers ~19 points for $0.04 (Haiku 5.5 `low`, 18.6 for $0.044, and `high`, 29.9 for $0.16, sit marginally on it - Terra `low` scores 29.4 for $0.07); **Terra covers 29-42 for $0.07-0.26** (`low`/`medium`/`high`; its `xhigh`, 45.3 for $0.40, is on it by a cent - 6.2 Sol `medium` scores 52.7 for $0.41); **6.2 Sol covers 41-57 for $0.18-1.01** (every level); **6.2 Astra `high`/`xhigh`/`max` covers 60-62 for $2.07-4.00**; then **Fable 5.2 `xhigh` (62.2, $14.83) and `max` (64.6, $26.54)**, the only rows above Astra `max`, at 3.7× and 6.6× its cost. **Everything else is off it.** 6.2 Sol `max` (57.3, $1.01) beats 6.2 Astra `medium` (57.0, $1.62) and Fable 5.2 `medium` (56.4, $3.80), and is within 0.1 of Opus 5.5 `xhigh` (57.4, $6.05) at a sixth of the cost; 6.2 Astra `high` (60.1, $2.07) beats Carbon `high` (60.0, $5.48 even at the intro price) and Opus 5.5 `max` (59.1, $10.04); 6.2 Astra `max` (62.1, $4.00) beats Fable 5.2 `high` (61.8, $9.08). **Gemini 4 Hydrogen is off it at every level** (6.2 Sol `medium` scores 52.7 for $0.41 vs Hydrogen `high` 50.8 for $1.87).
- **GPT-6.2 Sol is the value leader by a wide margin, a class above the model it replaces:** `max` (57.3, $1.01) beats Sonnet 5.5 `max` (54.5, $10.02) at a tenth of the cost and sits within ~3 points of Opus 5.5 `max`, Carbon `high` and 6.2 Astra `high` for a tenth, a fifth and half of their cost per task (Fable 5.2 `max` scores 7 points more at ~26× the cost); `medium` (52.7, $0.41) already beats Opus 5.5 `high` (51.8, $3.46), Hydrogen `high` (50.8, $1.87) and Argon `high` (50.4, $4.97). Against GPT-6.1 Sol in the same pull it scores 2.7-6.4 points more at every level for about the same cost (`max` 57.3 for $1.01 vs 54.6 for $1.27) in 25-66% less estimated generation time (474 s vs 1,398 s at `max`).
- **Effort matters more in v5 than it did in v4.3.** Opus 5.5 spans 35 → 59 from `low` to `max` at 10× the cost, Sonnet 5.5 26 → 55, 6.2 Sol 41 → 57, 6.2 Astra 46 → 62. The 6.2 models get most of it early: 6.2 Astra reaches 57.0 at `medium` and 60.1 at `high` (`max` adds 2.0 points for about twice the cost of `high`), and 6.2 Sol reaches 52.7 at `medium`. Fable 5.2 spans 54 → 65 and gets most of it by its default `high` (61.8; `max` adds 2.8 points for ~3× the cost). The flat curves are Carbon (54 → 60), Grok 4.7 (31 → 36), and Fable 5.1, whose `xhigh` (50.0) beat its `max` (49.2). So the old "sweep effort first" rule now has a larger payoff, and a model's default effort can understate it badly (Opus 5.5 `medium` is 48.2, Sonnet 5.5 `high` is 41.5).
- **Claude Sonnet 5.5 is cheap per token, not per task, at high effort.** `max` costs about what Opus 5.5 `max` costs ($10.02 vs $10.04) for 4.6 fewer points, and `high` (41.5, $1.64) costs nearly 3× GPT-6.2 Sol `high` (54.9, $0.56). Its case is speed (135-147 tok/s), Claude-specific behavior, and ZDR; if you need it at `xhigh`+, price Opus 5.5 `high`/`xhigh` first.
- **Claude Fable 5.2 keeps the top score, but only its `xhigh` and `max` are above GPT-6.2 Astra.** `max` (64.6, $26.54, ~274K output tokens per task) is 2.5 points above Astra `max` and the most expensive run in the table; its default `high` (61.8, $9.08, ~98K output tokens) now sits just under Astra `max` (62.1, $4.00, ~41K output tokens) and level with Astra `xhigh` (61.6, $2.52). It hallucinates like the other big Claude and GPT models (56-60%), its estimated generation times are long at the top (892-2,467 s), and it needs 30-day retention by default. Its predecessor **Fable 5.1 is superseded on AA** (best `xhigh` 50.0 at $10.85, below Argon and Terra `max`).
- **Gemini is strong but token-hungry per task.** Argon `high` uses ~116K output tokens per task (2.8× GPT-6.2 Astra `max`) and costs $4.97 at the intro price ($9.95 at list), and its default `medium` saves little (~100K tokens, $4.31, for 47.6); Carbon uses ~123K (29% fewer than Opus 5.5 `max`). **Carbon's `low` is not cheaper than its `medium`** (54.2 at $5.22 vs 58.3 at $4.68) and scores lower. **Gemini 4 Hydrogen is among the fastest per token** (158-172 tok/s; only Flash-Lite and Haiku 5.5 are faster, and Sonnet 5.5 is next at 135-147) and estimated generation times are moderate (501 s at its default `high`), but per task it costs more than GPT-6.2 Sol at the same score; its `high` (50.8) edges Argon (50.4) for less than half the cost. **Gemini 3.8 Flash is fast but not cheap per task** (30.8 for $7.77 even at intro prices - Hydrogen `medium` scores 41.2 for $0.80, GPT-6.1 Terra `low` 29.4 for $0.07).
- **Time frontier (shortest estimated generation time per level): the GPT-6.2 models hold it from 41 to 62 points**, because they finish a task in far fewer output tokens (6.2 Astra `high`: ~18K per task, against ~98K for Fable 5.2 `high`). In order: GPT-6 Luna `low` (37 s, 9.7), Terra `low` (72 s, 29.4), 6.2 Sol `low` (85 s, 40.6), 6.2 Astra `low` (93 s, 45.7), 6.2 Sol `medium` (184 s, 52.7), then 6.2 Astra `medium`/`high`/`xhigh`/`max` (231 s for 57.0, 305 s for 60.1, 391 s for 61.6, 689 s for 62.1); above that only Fable 5.2 `xhigh` (1,443 s, 62.2) and `max` (2,467 s, 64.6). Fable 5.2 is about twice as fast per token (110-113 tok/s against 6.2 Astra's 53-60) but needs 892 s for 61.8 at `high`, where Astra `xhigh` takes 391 s for 61.6. Hydrogen is fast per token but not on this frontier (`high` takes 501 s for 50.8; 6.2 Sol `medium` takes 184 s for 52.7). Kimi K3.1 Pro is the slowest per token (31 tok/s, 2,305 s per task; only Fable 5.2 `max` takes longer per task). These are generation-time estimates under AA's test load; tool calls add to them.
- **Hallucination varies by model and effort, not just intelligence.** Gemini dominates the low end: **Argon 4% at `high` (but 15% at its default `medium`), Carbon 4% at `medium` and 11% at `high`** (20% at `low`), **Hydrogen 8-12% from `medium` up** (19% at `low`), then Grok 4.7 (20%), Haiku 4.5 (23%), Flash-Lite (27%), Haiku 5.5 (34-40%), Terra (35-42%), and Kimi K3.1 Pro (37%). The big Claude and GPT models answer instead of abstaining far more often (6.2 Astra 54-59%, 6.2 Sol 55-66%, Fable 5.2 56-60%, Opus 5.5 50-67%; both 6.2 models hallucinate more than the models they replace - GPT-6 Astra 43-50%, 6.1 Sol 48-52%), and Luna guesses most (79-85%) - pair those with retrieval and an explicit "say you don't know" instruction.

AA v5 sub-benchmarks where published (Nov 7 pull unless noted, so before Fable 5.2 and GPT-6.2 - "Astra" in this paragraph is GPT-6 Astra): **FrontierCode 2.0** (Cognition's maintainer-merge coding test) - Opus 5.5 `max` 46% leads, then Astra `max`, Terra `max`, and Sonnet 5.5 `max` at 42%, Carbon 41%; **Terminal-Bench 4.0** - Kimi K3.1 Pro 67%, Carbon 65%, Sonnet 5.5 `max` 64%; **Terminal-Bench-Science** - Carbon 65%, Astra `max` 63%; **HLE-Diamond** - Carbon 69%, Opus 5.5 `max` 68%; **CritPt** (hard subset) - Astra 72%, Carbon 69%; **AA-Briefcase** (agentic knowledge work, launch figures) - Sonnet 5.5 led at 1,812 Elo, Carbon 1,609; **AA-Omniscience v1.1** - Carbon 54, Opus 5.5 `max` 48, Argon 46.

## What each is good at

- **Claude Fable 5.2** (Nov 9) - **AA v5 #1** (64.6 at `max`; its default `high`, 61.8 for $9.08, is now just under GPT-6.2 Astra `max`, 62.1 for $4.00). Anthropic reports it ahead of Opus 5.5 on nearly every benchmark it ran (FrontierCode 2.0 49.1% vs 45.5% at `xhigh`, Terminal-Bench-Science 74.6% vs 58.7%, Remote Labor Index 41.3% vs 21.3%; Opus 5.5 keeps the higher AA-Briefcase Elo). **#1 on the Agent arena** (Nov 11, max: 30.5% net improvement, a clear lead) and **#1 on GDPx** (16.4% at $88.15 per task, read Nov 18; 24.4% on its software-engineering domain); BullshitBench 62-66% clear pushback. $8/$40 (cache reads 0.025×), **30-day retention by default** (ZDR only by Anthropic's authorization; AWS offers it to eligible Bedrock customers for internal use through at least June 30 2027), long turns at `high`+. Anthropic still says start on Opus 5.5 and move up when your evals at higher effort fall short.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon** (**GA Nov 10**, paid tier; `gemini-4-carbon`) - **AA v5 #3** (60.0 at `high`; `medium`, the default, 58.3 for $4.68 at the intro price - GPT-6.2 Astra `high` now scores 60.1 for $2.07), with the lowest hallucination rates among top scorers (4% at `medium`) and led HLE-Diamond and Terminal-Bench-Science on AA's Nov 7 pull (Anthropic reports Fable 5.2 at 74.6% on Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1); **#3 on Arena's Agent arena** (Nov 11, preliminary) and **#3 on GDPx** (10.9% at $16.29 per task, a third of Opus 5.5's cost); BullshitBench 61-72% clear pushback. Google claims ~2× fewer unsupported claims than Argon and 0.0% prompt-injection success on Gray Swan's benchmark. $2 / $10 through Nov 30, then $4 / $20 - price it at $4 / $20 for anything that runs past November (AA's API already does: $6.59-7.76 per task). Slow, long turns (1,731-2,051 s estimated per task, 48-60 tok/s).
- **Claude Opus 5.5** - Anthropic's recommended default and the **strongest Claude with standard ZDR eligibility** (Fable 5.2 gets ZDR only by authorization or AWS's eligible-customer offer; across vendors GPT-6.2 Astra, ZDR-eligible for approved organizations, now scores higher on AA): AA v5 #4 (59.1 at `max`), **#2 on the Agent arena** (Nov 11, behind Fable 5.2) and **#2 on GDPx** (12.0%, behind Fable 5.2's 16.4%), and the FrontierCode 2.0 leader on AA's Nov 7 pull. Anthropic reports Fable-5.1-level work at ~40% lower cost than Opus 5, stronger long-running coding, knowledge work with fewer invented figures, and the lowest prompt-injection rate it has measured (tied with Fable 5.1). Its default `medium` (48.2) is far below its ceiling on v5 - raise effort for hard work. $4/$20, ZDR-eligible. BullshitBench 62-63% clear pushback.
- **GPT-6.2 Astra** (Nov 17; `gpt-6.2-astra`) - OpenAI's flagship and **AA v5 #2** (62.1 at `max` for $4.00; 60.1 at `high` for $2.07, above Carbon's and Opus 5.5's best at a fraction of their cost per task), on the cost and time frontiers from `high` up because it finishes a task in few output tokens (~18K at `high`). $8 / $40, 20% below GPT-6 Astra, with 0.05× cache reads. OpenAI reports new highs on Agents' Last Exam and Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 and slides, spreadsheets and documents that follow a supplied template more closely. The catches: it hallucinates more than GPT-6 Astra on AA (54-59% vs 43-50%), pushes back on false premises less (BullshitBench 49-55% vs 64-69%), leaves obstacles out of its reports more often on OpenAI's own tests, and refuses more security and biology work (all in `gpt.md`). Not yet on the Agent arena or GDPx (GPT-6 Astra: #7 on the Agent arena, #4 on GDPx overall and #2 once the safety-classifier domains are excluded).
- **GPT-6.2 Sol** (Nov 17; `gpt-6.2-sol`) - OpenAI's go-to model and **the value leader on AA v5**: on the cost frontier at every effort (`medium` 52.7 for $0.41, `max` 57.3 for $1.01) and #5 by best score, ahead of Sonnet 5.5 and Hydrogen. Same $2 / $10 as 6.1 Sol (cached input $0.10). OpenAI reports it within 0.5 points of 6.2 Astra on FrontierCode 2.0 at about a fifth of the cost (both at `max`). No `none` effort. The catches: a higher hallucination rate than 6.1 Sol (55-66% vs 48-52%) and the weakest false-premise pushback of the current GPT-6 models (BullshitBench 42-46%; 6.1 Sol 59-65%). Not yet on the Agent arena or GDPx (6.1 Sol: #9 on the Agent arena, #7 on GDPx).
- **Claude Sonnet 5.5** - fast (135-147 tok/s) at $2/$10 per token, reaching 54.5 at `max` and **leading AA-Briefcase**; **tied for #2 on GDPx's software-engineering domain** (11.1%, with Carbon, behind Fable 5.2's 24.4% and above Opus 5.5's 8.9% - a 15-task set, so one or two solved attempts apart). But it's **token-hungry at high effort**, so per *task* it isn't cheap there (see the curves). Best at `low`-`high` for speed-sensitive Claude workloads; Anthropic reports it above Opus 5.5 on Terminal-Bench 4.0 while Opus 5.5 stays stronger on open-ended work needing sustained judgment. #4 on the Agent arena (Nov 11). ZDR-eligible.
- **GPT-6.1 Terra** (Oct 26) - OpenAI's mid tier at $1 / $5 (cached $0.05): **on the cost frontier from 29 to 42 points** (`low`-`high`), and `max` (51.3, $0.96) is off it (6.2 Sol `medium` scores 52.7 for $0.41); short estimated generation times (72-253 s at `low`-`high`), strong at computer use and concise writing from supplied material, keeps `none` effort; #10 on the Agent arena (Nov 11).
- **Gemini 4 Argon** (GA Oct 13) - Google's efficient frontier model, "for the majority of software engineering and knowledge work": 50.4 on AA v5 at `high` and 47.6 at its default `medium` (a row AA added after Nov 13), **the lowest hallucination rate measured at `high` (4%, tied with Carbon `medium`) but 15% at `medium`**, led AutomationBench-AA at v5 launch, strong long-context and video (Google-reported). Token-hungry and slow to first token; $2 / $10 until Nov 30, then $4 / $20 ($4.97 per task at the intro price, $9.95 at list). ## Beyond coding - professional-domain agent work (SemiAnalysis GDPx)

[GDPx](https://gdpx.semianalysis.com/) (v1.0, Nov 2 2026) is the third reference, for **long agentic work in professional domains other than coding**: 206 tasks across 11 domains (RSI, chip design, cybersecurity, biology, finance, legal, quant trading, hardware design, materials science, alignment, software engineering) from 14 specialist data partners, run in live environments (real repos, a replica PE deal room, an EHR, lab-instrument simulators) with up to **8 hours per task**, in each model's native harness (Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity; OpenCode otherwise). Domains are weighted equally (and partners within a domain), grading is binary or continuous depending on the domain, the default score averages 3 attempts, and $/task is published. It is a true frontier benchmark - the best model scores 16%.

**Overall board, read Nov 18 2026** (score, $/task): **Claude Fable 5.2 16.4% ($88.15)**, **Claude Opus 5.5 12.0% ($51.41)**, **Gemini 4 Carbon 10.9% ($16.29)**, GPT-6 Astra 9.6% ($53.99), Muse Wonder Full 9.0% (no price), Claude Fable 5.1 8.5% ($67.16), GPT-6.1 Sol 8.4% ($9.80), Claude Sonnet 5.5 8.2% ($45.27), Gemini 4 Argon 7.3% ($12.79), Muse Wonder 6.8% ($15.63), DeepSeek 4.1 Flash 5.4% ($0.94), GPT-6 Luna 5.1% ($0.92), Grok 4.7 3.9% ($16.34), GLM 5.3 2.7%. Fable 5.2 is the only addition since the Nov 7 read; the GPT-6.2 models are not on it yet. Things to know before using it:

- **Safety classifiers move the ranking.** SemiAnalysis says Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra are "sandbagged" by their classifiers in cyber, biology, and alignment; the site's **"Exclude domains affected by safety classifiers"** board (8 domains) keeps Fable 5.2 first (16.8%) and reorders the rest: **GPT-6 Astra 11.8%, Opus 5.5 11.7%, GPT-6.1 Sol 10.6%, Sonnet 5.5 9.7%, Carbon 9.2%**, Fable 5.1 8.6%. Carbon's overall #3 leans on biology, which SemiAnalysis attributes "likely" to weaker safety classifiers. Pick the board that matches whether your domain trips classifiers.
- **Finance and legal are effectively unsolved.** Under the intended (binary) grading every model scores 0% on legal, and on finance only Carbon scores above zero (2.8%); the site's "Partial score" metric shows the continuous credit.
- **Domains are spiky.** Per SemiAnalysis's launch write-up (before Fable 5.2 was added), Opus 5.5 wins quant trading, hardware design, and chip design; GPT-6 Astra wins RSI and materials science; Carbon wins biology and edges software engineering. **On the software-engineering domain (Datacurve, 15 tasks) Fable 5.2 now leads with 24.4%; Claude Sonnet 5.5 ties Carbon at 11.1% and ranks above Opus 5.5 (8.9%, tied with GPT-6.1 Sol and Argon)** - with 15 tasks × 3 attempts that is about one solved attempt, so read it as "Sonnet 5.5 at `max` is not behind Opus 5.5 on this kind of work," not as a lead.
- **Caveats:** a commercial SemiAnalysis product built with data vendors that sell to the labs, its domains picked to match SemiAnalysis's own AI-revenue demand model; mostly private partner tasks (one public task per partner); not independently replicated; one evaluator's harness choices. Directional, like the others.

## Beyond intelligence - false-premise detection (BullshitBench)

A capability AA, Arena, and GDPx miss: **does the model call out a nonsensical or false premise instead of building on it?** On [BullshitBench](https://petergpt.github.io/bullshit-benchmark/) (v2: 100 nonsense prompts, 13 techniques, 5 domains, models run with no system prompt; clear-pushback rate over **all attempts** from `leaderboard.csv`, repo pulled 2026-09-23 - the dashboard's headline instead **excludes refusals** from the denominator, so heavy refusers such as Fable 5.x read ~10 points higher there): **Claude Opus 4.8 is still #1 at ~95%**; the Claude 5 generation is lower - **Sonnet 5 ~80%, Fable 5.1 64-77%, Opus 5 70-73%**; **GPT-6 Astra jumped to 64-69%** (GPT-5.6 Sol ~47%, Terra ~47-54%, Luna ~37-41%); **Gemini 3.7 Flash ~31-35%**. The durable lessons hold: **raising effort doesn't help and often hurts** (Fable 5.1: 77% at `low` vs 64% at `max`), and **refusal ≠ pushback** (Fable 5.x declines 11-36% of prompts instead of naming the flaw). **v2 additions through Oct 13 (clear-pushback rate):** Opus 5.5 62-63%, Sonnet 5.5 58-60%, **Haiku 5.5 53-57%** (Haiku 4.5: 77%), GPT-6.1 Sol 59-65%, GPT-6 Sol 54-59%, GPT-6 Luna 58-59%, **Gemini 4 Argon 65-66%** (but 13-15% outright acceptance vs ~4% for Opus 5.5 / GPT-6 Astra), Kimi K3 44-58%, Grok 4.6 56-66% - and again **higher effort scored lower** for Argon, Kimi K3, and Grok 4.3/4.6. The whole Claude 5.5 family pushes back less than Opus 4.8 / Haiku 4.5 did. **v2 additions on Nov 18 (clear-pushback rate at the lowest and highest effort run):** **Gemini 4 Hydrogen 62% at `low` and 90% at `xhigh`** (the best current model), Grok 4.7 75% and 72%, Gemini 4 Carbon 61% and 72%, Claude Fable 5.2 62% and 66% (2% refusals), Kimi K3.1 Pro 50% and 60%, **GPT-6.2 Astra 49% and 55%** (GPT-6 Astra: 64-69%), GPT-6.1 Terra 54% and 53%, **GPT-6.2 Sol 46% and 42%** (6.1 Sol: 59-65%; it accepted the nonsense outright on 17-25% of prompts). So both GPT-6.2 models push back less than the models they replace, and in this batch higher effort scored higher for five of the eight models (Hydrogen by 28 points) - the effort rule is a default to test, not a law. Not yet benchmarked: Gemini 3.8 Flash. If your app must not act on broken user premises, weight this - and fix it with explicit prompting + model choice, not the effort knob.

## Picking a model - decision rules

- **Highest ceiling** → **Claude Fable 5.2** (AA v5 #1; its default `high` scores 61.8 for $9.08, less than Opus 5.5 `max`) when 30-day retention is acceptable (or you have Anthropic's ZDR authorization, or qualify for AWS's ZDR offer on Bedrock) and turns can run long - Anthropic still recommends starting on Opus 5.5 and moving up when your evals at higher effort fall short; **Claude Opus 5.5** (AA v5 #4 at `max`, #2 on the Agent arena and on GDPx, $4/$20, ZDR) as the default top pick on Claude and the strongest Claude under standard ZDR; **Gemini 4 Carbon** (GA Nov 10) is AA v5 #3, #3 on the Agent arena and #3 on GDPx at about a third of Opus's GDPx cost per task - price it at the $4 / $20 it costs from Dec 1, and expect long turns; **GPT-6.2 Astra** (AA v5 #2: 62.1 at `max` for $4.00, 60.1 at `high` for $2.07) when you want near-top scores at a fraction of the cost per task and generation time, OpenAI's stack, computer use, or template-faithful documents - add the blocker-disclosure and premise-check lines from `gpt.md`, and expect refusals on security and biology work; **Gemini 4 Argon** when you want Google's stack, the lowest hallucination rate (set `high`: 4%, vs 15% at its default `medium`), or long-context/video work (price doubles Dec 1); **Kimi K3.1 Pro** when you want open weights (released Nov 4) at the top of the open-weight band.
- **Claude, Opus 5.5 or Fable 5.2** → price them per task, not per token: on AA v5, Fable 5.2 `low` (53.9, $3.10) beats Opus 5.5 `high` (51.8, $3.46), and Fable 5.2 `medium` (56.4, $3.80) is within a point of Opus 5.5 `xhigh` (57.4, $6.05) for ~37% less. The catches: 30-day retention by default instead of ZDR, a separate rate limit, longer turns, and prompts that need the Fable 5.2 review in `claude-fable.md`.
- **Default agentic coding** → **Opus 5.5** (FrontierCode 2.0 leader on AA's Nov 7 pull; Anthropic reports Fable 5.2 higher), starting at its `medium` default and raising effort for hard tasks (v5 rewards effort: `high` 51.8, `xhigh` 57.4); for the best capability-per-dollar, **GPT-6.2 Sol** (`medium`-`xhigh`: 52.7-55.7 on AA v5 for $0.41-0.65 per task; OpenAI reports it within 0.5 points of 6.2 Astra on FrontierCode 2.0 at `max`); on Claude, Sonnet 5.5 at `medium` for well-specified tasks (fast) - at `xhigh`+ Opus 5.5 is usually cheaper per task, though Sonnet 5.5 at `max` tied Carbon for second on GDPx's software-engineering domain, above Opus 5.5 (Fable 5.2 leads that domain by a wide margin).
- **Everyday business / support / internal tools** → **GPT-6.2 Sol** (`low`/`medium`) or **GPT-6.1 Terra** (`medium`/`high`), or **Sonnet 5.5** (`low`/`medium`) when you need Claude behavior.
- **Fast multimodal / agentic work at scale** → **Gemini 4 Hydrogen** (`medium` or `high`; it replaces 3.8 Flash) when throughput and per-token speed are the constraint, alone or as the execution model under Carbon or Argon; if cost per task is the constraint, GPT-6.2 Sol or GPT-6.1 Terra costs less for the same AA score.
- **High-volume classification / routing / extraction** → **GPT-6 Luna** (or OpenAI's **Decisions API** for typed answers with free output tokens), **Gemini Decision 1** (Google's Decisions API, Nov 11: calibrated yes/no, choice and score answers, zero output tokens, $0.10 per 1M input - `gemini.md`), or **Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite**; on Claude, **Haiku 5.5** at `low`/`medium` (keep prompts under 100K tokens).
- **Mid tier on OpenAI** → **GPT-6.1 Terra** (on the AA cost frontier from 29 to 42 points) for computer use, concise writing, and scoped fixes; GPT-6.2 Sol for harder coding and agentic work.
- **Live X/Twitter data** → Grok 4.7 with server-side `x_search`.
- **Strict instruction-following / adherence** → Claude 4.7+ (literal following) or GPT-6. (Gemini 3.1 Pro led IFBench on AA's old index, which v5 no longer reports; Google itself points Pro-tier work to Argon.)
- **Professional-domain agent work beyond coding** (finance, legal, science, hardware, security) → check the matching GDPx domain board, not the overall score: rankings flip by domain and by whether safety classifiers apply, and finance/legal are unsolved by every model.
- **Must not build on false premises** → Opus 4.8 still leads by far; among current models **Gemini 4 Hydrogen at `xhigh` (90%)** leads, then Grok 4.7 (72-75%), Carbon at `high` (72%), Argon (65-66%) and Fable 5.2 (62-66%); **GPT-6.2 Astra (49-55%) and GPT-6.2 Sol (42-46%) score below the models they replace**, so add an explicit premise-check instruction when you move to them. Don't count on the 5.5-generation Claude models here without one either.
- **Data retention constraints** → Fable 5.x requires 30-day retention by default (unless Anthropic authorizes ZDR; for Fable 5.2, AWS also offers eligible Bedrock customers ZDR for internal use through at least June 30 2027); Opus 5.5 and the GPT-6 models (6.2 Astra and Sol included) are ZDR-eligible; Gemini 4 models follow the Gemini API's tier terms (paid-tier data isn't used to improve Google's products; Carbon has no free tier); Gemini's Interactions API stores 55 days by default (set `store=false`; new paid `generateContent` projects also store by default since Oct 26); xAI stores Responses 30 days by default (team-wide ZDR disables stateful features); Moonshot offers ZDR to enterprise customers.
- **Before combining models, sweep effort on one.** Anthropic's measurements: a frontier model at *low* effort often beats a cheaper model at its default on cost per solved task, and in every measured case where the work fit one context window and had no cost tail, the lead model alone at lower effort was cheaper than an orchestrator. Two multi-model shapes earn their keep: an **advisor** (cheap executor consults a frontier model on hard decisions - only pays if the executor actually consults, which low effort can suppress) and an **orchestrator** (frontier lead, cheaper workers - pays as insurance against a routine-task cost tail, or when input exceeds one context window).
- **Routing inside an agent loop** → worth it only if the price gap pays for the router. LangChain + NVIDIA Switchyard (Aug 2026): on a 145-task agent suite, escalation routing between a 30B open model and Claude Opus 4.8 sent ~7% of calls to Opus (which still carried 68% of spend), cutting cost 74% for ~6 points of accuracy; the router's judge was 21% of routed spend because it gets no cache benefit. Break-even: **minimum offload share = judge cost ÷ (expensive-model cost − cheap-model cost)** per run - if your two models are close in price this exceeds 100% and routing can't pay (unless the cheap model is self-hosted). Budget for a range, since escalation rates vary run to run; skip routing on latency-critical or short single-turn workloads.
- **Multi-tier pipeline** (the usual pattern): cheap tier for extract-classify-route (Luna / Flash-Lite / Haiku 5.5) → frontier tier for analysis and generation (Opus 5.5 / Sol / Astra / Fable 5.2 / Argon). Google's own recommended split is now Carbon or Argon as orchestrator and verifier with Hydrogen workers (its Antigravity Orchestrator switches between Carbon and Hydrogen automatically); Anthropic's is Opus 5.5 or Sonnet 5.5 with Haiku 5.5 subagents. See SKILL.md Section D.

## Cost-per-task ≠ per-token price

Compare **cost per task at your effort setting**, not headline $/1M. Token efficiency now varies a lot: Opus 5.5 finishes tasks in fewer tokens than Opus 5, GPT-6.2 Astra uses ~41K output tokens per AA task at `max` where GPT-6 Astra used ~52K and Fable 5.2 uses ~274K, while Gemini 3.8 Flash deliberately spends *more* tokens (self-verification) - its low per-token price overstates its advantage on long agentic tasks. Haiku 5.5 and Kimi K3.1 Pro), 0.25× on Grok 4.6/4.7 (variants differ - Kimi Highspeed 0.05×, Grok 4.5 0.15×; check the provider file). ## Methodology notes

AA's **Intelligence Index v5** (Oct 27 2026) is built from nine evaluations in four equally weighted pillars: **Knowledge Work** 25% (AA-Briefcase 15%, AutomationBench-AA 5%, GDP.pdf 5%), **Coding** 25% (FrontierCode 2.0 15%, Terminal-Bench 4.0 10%), **Science** 25% (Terminal-Bench-Science 15%, a private hard subset of CritPt v2 10%), and **General** 25% (AA-Omniscience v1.1 15% - 10% accuracy, 5% non-hallucination - and HLE-Diamond 10%). 60% of the index now uses private test sets or held-out answers. New in v5: FrontierCode 2.0 and Terminal-Bench-Science; upgraded: HLE-Diamond (replacing Humanity's Last Exam), AA-Omniscience v1.1, CritPt v2; removed: GDPval-AA, SciCode, and AA-LCR. v5 tasks are far more agentic (median output tokens per task rose from 35K to 59K), which is why effort and token appetite move cost per task so much more than on v4.3. Arena's Agent arena scores "net improvement" from human votes on real agent sessions. GDPx averages pass@1 over 3 attempts per task in native harnesses with up to 8 hours per task. BullshitBench uses a 0/1/2 rubric (accepted nonsense / buried challenge / clear pushback) from a 3-judge panel over 100 prompts × 5 domains. All four are one evaluator's methodology - directional. A model's rank on a public benchmark rarely predicts its rank on *your* task (build your own evals - see `evals.md`).
