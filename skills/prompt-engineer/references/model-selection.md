# Model Selection & Cost (cross-provider)

> **Scope - read this when *choosing* a model, not when *prompting* one.** Which tier for a task, at what budget, good at what. For *how to write the prompt* once you've picked, use `claude.md` / `gpt.md` / `gemini.md` / `kimi.md` / `grok.md`, plus the per-model file SKILL.md Step 0b names for your model (`gemini-4.md` for a Gemini 4 model). Keep this file **out of the prompt-authoring context** - benchmark tables are low-signal noise while writing a prompt (the "context rot" caution in SKILL.md Section A applies to the skill itself).
>
> **Staleness - the numbers are a dated snapshot, the *relationships* are the durable part.** Sources: Artificial Analysis (Intelligence Index **v5.0.2**; every row re-pulled **2026-11-26**, eight days after Gemini 4 Neon launched), Arena (formerly LMArena) Agent arena (board dated **2026-11-22**), SemiAnalysis GDPx (**v1.0**, board read **2026-11-26**), BullshitBench (v2 data through **2026-11-26**). **AA moved to Intelligence Index v5 on 2026-10-27, and v5 scores are not comparable to v4.3** (or to the July or September snapshots) - v5 swapped most of the evals, so every model scores differently; never compare a v5 number with an older one. **Patch versions move scores too:** v5.0.2 (November) regraded FrontierCode and moved to Terminal-Bench 4.1, and 28 of the 60 rows already in this table changed by 0.1-0.3 points between Nov 18 and Nov 26. AA's live numbers also drift after launch (Opus 5.5 `max` was 58 in the v5 announcement and reads 59.1 now; Carbon launched at 59 and reads 60.1). **Measured speed drifts more than score:** GPT-6.2 Astra `max` read 689 s per task on Nov 18, the day after its launch, and 924 s on Nov 26 (every GPT-6.2 row reads 15-28% slower per token than in launch week), and Carbon `high` went from 1,702 s (Nov 13) to 2,051 s (Nov 18) to 2,374 s. **Costs can move with no price change** (see the column notes). **The tok/s column changed basis on 2026-11-17** (AA's Performance v3 speed instead of the older median - see the column notes below), so a tok/s figure here is not comparable with one quoted before that date. Releases land weekly; trust the **bands, gaps, and shape of the curves**, and re-pull before relying on a figure (recipe: `model-selection-refresh.md`).

## Intelligence, cost, and speed - per model and effort (AA Intelligence Index v5.0.2, pulled 2026-11-26)

Every effort level AA tests for the current models. **Intel** = AA Intelligence Index v5 (v5.0.2). **Cost / task** = AA's cost estimate per Intelligence Index task (the prices AA holds for the model × the tokens the run used, incl. reasoning, with cache reads and writes at AA's measured typical cache hit rate) - the number to compare, not $/1M, and an estimate, not what your workload will bill. **Gemini 4 Carbon and Argon are costed at their $4 / $20 list price**, which Google charges from Dec 1; through Nov 30 they bill at $2 / $10 (on Nov 18 AA's pages still costed Carbon at the intro price: $4.68 per task at `medium`, $5.48 at `high`). Gemini 3.8 Flash is at its $0.75 / $3.75 intro price. **A cost can move with no price change:** AA combines token counts with "live measurements of the model's typical cache hit rate", and between Nov 18 and Nov 26 Gemini 3.8 Flash `high` went from $7.77 to $3.02 per task and Hydrogen `low` from $0.29 to $0.37 at unchanged token prices. **Time / task** = AA's *estimated* generation time per task: "output tokens per task divided by output speed, weighted by each benchmark's weight in the Intelligence Index" - it leaves out tool execution, and it reflects the load on AA's test requests, not yours, so a full agent run takes longer (v5 tasks are much longer and more agentic than v4.3's). **tok/s** = AA's headline output speed under its Performance v3 method (Nov 3 2026; `performance_v3.mean_output_tokens_per_second` in the API): output tokens, reasoning included, per second over each request's full duration - so the wait for the first token counts against it - averaged over 1K-, 10K- and 100K-token prompts and multi-turn agentic tasks, run under concurrent load, across the past 5 days, with requests that fail on the provider's side counted as 0. It is the speed behind Time / task: output tokens per task ÷ tok/s reproduced AA's time on every row on Nov 17 and on Nov 26 (on Nov 18 one day-old row, GPT-6.2 Astra `medium`, was 8% off). **Until 2026-11-17 this column held the older median** (10K-token prompts only; AA stops publishing it on Dec 10 2026). By the new measure the Claude models read 10-49% faster and the Gemini 4 models 12-35% slower than by the old one, so don't mix the two. AA counts tokens as each provider's API reports them, so a token is not the same unit across vendors: compare Time / task across vendors and tok/s within one. **Halluc.** = AA-Omniscience v1.1 hallucination rate (answers wrong instead of abstaining on knowledge it lacks; lower is better - Haiku and Flash-Lite score low partly by abstaining, not by knowing more). "-" = not published. Ordered by each model's best score. OpenAI documents no API default effort for the GPT-6.2 models (it says to start at `medium`), so none is marked. Superseded models and models without a full set of v5 rows are omitted (GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol, which the 6.2 models replaced here on Nov 18 - their last rows are quoted under *Superseded* below - GPT-6 Sol, GPT-5.6 Terra, Claude Fable 5.1 - best v5 row `xhigh` 50.0 at $10.85 per task - Kimi K3, and Gemini 3.1 Pro; `gpt-6.md` quotes 5.6 Terra's v5 `max` row for comparison).

### What the curves say

- **Cost frontier (most intelligence per dollar): GPT-6 Luna → GPT-6.1 Terra → GPT-6.2 Sol → GPT-6.2 Astra → Claude Fable 5.2 `max`.** OpenAI's models hold it from 10 to 62 points, apart from two marginal Claude Haiku 5.5 rows. Luna `medium` covers ~19 points for $0.04 (Haiku 5.5 `low`, 18.6 for $0.044, and `high`, 30.0 for $0.16, sit marginally on it - Terra `low` scores 29.4 for $0.07); **Terra covers 29-45 for $0.07-0.40** (`low` to `xhigh`; its `xhigh`, 45.3 for $0.40, is on it by a cent - 6.2 Sol `medium` scores 52.4 for $0.41); **6.2 Sol covers 41-57 for $0.18-1.01** (every level); **6.2 Astra `high`/`xhigh`/`max` covers 60-62 for $2.07-4.00**; then **Fable 5.2 `max` (64.8, $26.54)**, the only row above Astra `max`, at 6.6× its cost. **Everything else is off it, Gemini 4 Neon included:** Neon `high` (59.8, $3.74) and `xhigh` (61.5, $6.44) score just under 6.2 Astra `high` (60.3, $2.07) and `xhigh` (61.8, $2.52) for 1.8× and 2.6× the cost. Fable 5.2 `xhigh` (62.2, $14.83) now ties 6.2 Astra `max` (62.2, $4.00) at 3.7× the cost, and Fable 5.2 `high` (61.9, $9.08) sits 0.3 below it. 6.2 Sol `max` (57.3, $1.01) beats 6.2 Astra `medium` (57.0, $1.62) and Fable 5.2 `medium` (56.3, $3.80), and is within 0.2 of Opus 5.5 `xhigh` (57.5, $6.05) at a sixth of the cost; 6.2 Astra `high` beats Carbon `high` (60.1, $7.76) and Opus 5.5 `max` (59.1, $10.04). **Within Google's own line, Neon beats Carbon one level down:** Neon `high` beats Carbon `medium` (58.4, $6.59) on score, cost and time, and Neon `xhigh` beats Carbon `high` on all three. **Gemini 4 Hydrogen is off the frontier at every level** (6.2 Sol `medium` scores 52.4 for $0.41 vs Hydrogen `high` 51.0 for $1.87).
- **GPT-6.2 Sol is the value leader by a wide margin:** `max` (57.3, $1.01) beats Sonnet 5.5 `max` (54.7, $10.02) at a tenth of the cost and sits within ~3 points of Opus 5.5 `max`, Carbon `high`, Neon `high` and 6.2 Astra `high` for a tenth, an eighth, about a quarter and half of their cost per task (Fable 5.2 `max` scores 7.5 points more at ~26× the cost); `medium` (52.4, $0.41) already beats Opus 5.5 `high` (51.8, $3.46), Hydrogen `high` (51.0, $1.87) and Argon `high` (50.5, $7.06). It replaced GPT-6.1 Sol in this table on Nov 18, when in the same pull it scored 2.7-6.4 points more at every level for about the same cost.
- **Effort matters more in v5 than it did in v4.3.** Opus 5.5 spans 35 → 59 from `low` to `max` at 10× the cost, Sonnet 5.5 26 → 55, 6.2 Sol 41 → 57, 6.2 Astra 46 → 62, Neon 44 → 61.5 (`low` to `xhigh`, at 7× the cost). The 6.2 models get most of it early: 6.2 Astra reaches 57.0 at `medium` and 60.3 at `high` (`max` adds 1.9 points for about twice the cost of `high`), and 6.2 Sol reaches 52.4 at `medium`. Neon's last step is the expensive one: `xhigh` adds 1.7 points over `high` for 72% more cost and 85% more time. Fable 5.2 spans 54 → 65 and gets most of it by its default `high` (61.9; `max` adds 2.9 points for ~3× the cost). The flat curves are Carbon (54 → 60), Grok 4.7 (31 → 35), and Fable 5.1, whose `xhigh` (50.0) beat its `max` (49.2). So the old "sweep effort first" rule now has a larger payoff, and a model's default effort can understate it badly (Opus 5.5 `medium` is 48.3, Sonnet 5.5 `high` is 41.5).
- **Claude Sonnet 5.5 is cheap per token, not per task, at high effort.** `max` costs about what Opus 5.5 `max` costs ($10.02 vs $10.04) for 4.4 fewer points, and `high` (41.5, $1.64) costs nearly 3× GPT-6.2 Sol `high` (54.8, $0.56). Its case is speed (134-153 tok/s), Claude-specific behavior, and ZDR; if you need it at `xhigh`+, price Opus 5.5 `high`/`xhigh` first.
- **Claude Fable 5.2 keeps the top score, but only its `max` is above GPT-6.2 Astra.** `max` (64.8, $26.54, ~274K output tokens per task) is 2.6 points above Astra `max` and the most expensive run in the table; its `xhigh` (62.2, $14.83) ties Astra `max` (62.2, $4.00, ~41K output tokens), and its default `high` (61.9, $9.08, ~98K output tokens) is level with Astra `xhigh` (61.8, $2.52) and just above Neon `xhigh` (61.5, $6.44). It hallucinates like the other big Claude and GPT models (56-60%), its estimated generation times are long at the top (881-2,422 s), and it needs 30-day retention by default. Its predecessor **Fable 5.1 is superseded on AA** (best `xhigh` 50.0 at $10.85, below Argon and Terra `max`).
- **Gemini 4 is strong but token-hungry per task, and Neon is its efficient point.** Neon `high` uses ~90K output tokens per task and `xhigh` ~142K (GPT-6.2 Astra `max`: ~41K); Carbon `high` uses ~123K and Argon `high` ~116K. On AA's index **Neon matches or beats Carbon for less**: Neon `xhigh` (61.5, $6.44, 1,637 s) beats Carbon `high` (60.1, $7.76, 2,374 s) on score, cost and time, by 17% on cost and 31% on time; Neon `high` (59.8, $3.74, 884 s) beats Carbon `medium` (58.4, $6.59, 1,700 s) on all three; and Neon `high` is 0.3 points under Carbon `high` for 48% of its cost and 37% of its time. Neon hallucinates somewhat more (14% against Carbon's 4% at `medium` and 11% at `high`). **Carbon's `low` is not cheaper than its `medium`** (54.2 at $7.19 vs 58.4 at $6.59) and scores lower, and **Argon's `medium` now costs more than its `high`** ($8.63 for 47.7 vs $7.06 for 50.5) - at list price Argon is the worst value in the Gemini 4 line (Hydrogen `high` scores 51.0 for $1.87). **Gemini 4 Hydrogen is among the fastest per token** (151-177 tok/s; only Flash-Lite and Haiku 5.5 are faster, and Sonnet 5.5 is next at 134-153) and estimated generation times are moderate (480 s at its default `high`), but per task it costs more than GPT-6.2 Sol at the same score. **Gemini 3.8 Flash scores 30.7 for $3.02 per task** (down from $7.77 on Nov 18 at the same token prices - see the column notes); Hydrogen `medium` scores 41.1 for $0.80 and GPT-6.1 Terra `low` 29.4 for $0.07.
- **Time frontier (shortest estimated generation time per level): the GPT-6.2 models hold it from 41 to 62 points**, because they finish a task in far fewer output tokens (6.2 Astra `high`: ~18K per task, against ~98K for Fable 5.2 `high` and ~90K for Neon `high`) - even though they now read 15-28% slower per token than in their launch week. In order: GPT-6 Luna `low` (33 s, 9.7), Terra `low` (73 s, 29.4), 6.2 Sol `low` (103 s, 40.6), 6.2 Astra `low` (129 s, 45.7), 6.2 Sol `medium` (223 s, 52.4), then 6.2 Astra `medium`/`high`/`xhigh` (295 s for 57.0, 404 s for 60.3, 510 s for 61.8), Fable 5.2 `high` (881 s, 61.9), 6.2 Astra `max` (924 s, 62.2) and Fable 5.2 `max` (2,422 s, 64.8). Fable 5.2 is about 2.5× as fast per token (110-113 tok/s against 6.2 Astra's 40-45) but needs 881 s for 61.9 at `high`, where Astra `xhigh` takes 510 s for 61.8. Neon is not on it: `high` takes 884 s for 59.8 (Carbon `high`: 2,374 s for 60.1) and `xhigh` 1,637 s for 61.5. Hydrogen is fast per token but not on this frontier either (`high` takes 480 s for 51.0; 6.2 Sol `medium` takes 223 s for 52.4). Kimi K3.1 Pro is the slowest per token (36 tok/s, 2,001 s per task; only Carbon `high` and Fable 5.2 `max` take longer per task). These are generation-time estimates under AA's test load; tool calls add to them.
- **Hallucination varies by model and effort, not just intelligence.** Gemini dominates the low end: **Argon 4% at `high` (but 15% at its default `medium`), Carbon 4% at `medium` and 11% at `high`** (20% at `low`), **Hydrogen 8-12% from `medium` up** (19% at `low`), **Neon 14-16% from `medium` up** (19% at `low`), then Grok 4.7 (20%; 34% at `low`), Haiku 4.5 (23%), Flash-Lite (27%), Haiku 5.5 (34-40%), Terra (35-42%), and Kimi K3.1 Pro (37%). The big Claude and GPT models answer instead of abstaining far more often (6.2 Astra 54-59%, 6.2 Sol 55-66%, Fable 5.2 56-60%, Opus 5.5 50-67%; both 6.2 models hallucinate more than the models they replace - GPT-6 Astra 43-50%, 6.1 Sol 48-52%), and Luna guesses most (79-85%) - pair those with retrieval and an explicit "say you don't know" instruction. Of the five models that score above 59, Neon and Carbon are the two that mostly abstain when they don't know.

AA v5 sub-benchmarks where published (Nov 7 pull unless noted, so before Fable 5.2, GPT-6.2 and Neon, and before v5.0.2 replaced Terminal-Bench 4.0 with 4.1 - "Astra" in this paragraph is GPT-6 Astra): **FrontierCode 2.0** (Cognition's maintainer-merge coding test) - Opus 5.5 `max` 46% leads, then Astra `max`, Terra `max`, and Sonnet 5.5 `max` at 42%, Carbon 41%; **Terminal-Bench 4.0** - Kimi K3.1 Pro 67%, Carbon 65%, Sonnet 5.5 `max` 64%; **Terminal-Bench-Science** - Carbon 65%, Astra `max` 63%; **HLE-Diamond** - Carbon 69%, Opus 5.5 `max` 68%; **CritPt** (hard subset) - Astra 72%, Carbon 69%; **AA-Briefcase** (agentic knowledge work, launch figures) - Sonnet 5.5 led at 1,812 Elo, Carbon 1,609; **AA-Omniscience v1.1** - Carbon 54, Opus 5.5 `max` 48, Argon 46.

## What each is good at

- **Claude Fable 5.2** (Nov 9) - **AA v5 #1** (64.8 at `max`; its default `high`, 61.9 for $9.08, is just under GPT-6.2 Astra `max`, 62.2 for $4.00). Anthropic reports it ahead of Opus 5.5 on nearly every benchmark it ran (FrontierCode 2.0 49.1% vs 45.5% at `xhigh`, Terminal-Bench-Science 74.6% vs 58.7%, Remote Labor Index 41.3% vs 21.3%; Opus 5.5 keeps the higher AA-Briefcase Elo). **#1 on the Agent arena** (Nov 22: index 1,449 at `max`, 107 points clear of Opus 5.5) and **#2 on GDPx** (16.4% at $88.15 per task, behind GPT-6.2 Astra's 19.3%; first on its software-engineering domain with 24.4%); BullshitBench 62-66% clear pushback. $8/$40 (cache reads 0.025×), **30-day retention by default** (ZDR only by Anthropic's authorization; AWS offers it to eligible Bedrock customers for internal use through at least June 30 2027), long turns at `high`+. Anthropic still says start on Opus 5.5 and move up when your evals at higher effort fall short.
- **GPT-6.2 Astra** (Nov 17; `gpt-6.2-astra`) - OpenAI's flagship and **AA v5 #2** (62.2 at `max` for $4.00; 60.3 at `high` for $2.07, above Carbon's and Opus 5.5's best at a fraction of their cost per task), on the cost and time frontiers from `high` up because it finishes a task in few output tokens (~18K at `high`). **#1 on GDPx** (19.3% at $46.76 per task, read Nov 26; 19.2% with the safety-classifier domains excluded) but **#8 on the Agent arena** (Nov 22: 1,275 at `max`, just below GPT-6 Astra's 1,284). $8 / $40, 20% below GPT-6 Astra, with 0.05× cache reads. OpenAI reports new highs on Agents' Last Exam and Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 and slides, spreadsheets and documents that follow a supplied template more closely. The catches: it hallucinates more than GPT-6 Astra on AA (54-59% vs 43-50%), pushes back on false premises less (BullshitBench 49-55% vs 64-69%), leaves obstacles out of its reports more often on OpenAI's own tests, and refuses more security and biology work (all in `gpt.md`).
- **Gemini 4 Neon** (**GA Nov 18**, paid tier; `gemini-4-neon`) - Google's middle Gemini 4 model, adapted from Carbon, and **AA v5 #3** (61.5 at `xhigh` for $6.44; 59.8 at its default `high` for $3.74): its `xhigh` is above Carbon's best on AA for 17% less per task and 31% less time, and its `high` is 0.3 points under Carbon's best for half the cost and 37% of the time, with a **low hallucination rate for a top scorer (14-19%; GPT-6.2 Astra 54-59%, Fable 5.2 56-60%)**. **#3 on the Agent arena** (Nov 22, preliminary: 1,331 at `xhigh`, $2.46 and 8 minutes per task at the median) and **#6 on GDPx** (10.1% at $29.04 per task, behind Carbon's 10.9% at $16.29; 4.4% on its software-engineering domain); BullshitBench 67% clear pushback at `low` and 84% at `xhigh`. $3 / $15 (cached input $0.15), no intro price. Google's model card (vendor-reported, Nov 2026) puts it first on 16 of 19 rows against Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.2 Sol (three of those rows show a result for only one of the two), including Agents' Last Exam v1.1 (75.8%), AutomationBench (56.3%), HLE-Diamond (71.3%) and GraphWalks at 256K-1M tokens (95.2% vs 66.8% and 80.1%), behind Opus 5.5 on Terminal-Bench 4.1 (65.2% vs 65.8%) and FrontierCode 2.0 (46.2% vs 47.3%), and behind GPT-6.2 Sol on Chartography; the card doesn't compare it with Fable 5.2, GPT-6.2 Astra or Carbon. It is off AA's cost and time frontiers (GPT-6.2 Astra `high` scores 60.3 for $2.07 in 404 s), so its case is Google's stack, a near-top score with far fewer confident wrong answers, long-context and multimodal work, and orchestrating Hydrogen workers.

## Beyond coding - professional-domain agent work (SemiAnalysis GDPx)

[GDPx](https://gdpx.semianalysis.com/) (v1.0, Nov 2 2026) is the third reference, for **long agentic work in professional domains other than coding**: 206 tasks across 11 domains (RSI, chip design, cybersecurity, biology, finance, legal, quant trading, hardware design, materials science, alignment, software engineering) from 14 specialist data partners, run in live environments (real repos, a replica PE deal room, an EHR, lab-instrument simulators) with up to **8 hours per task**, in each model's native harness (Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity; OpenCode otherwise). Domains are weighted equally (and partners within a domain), grading is binary or continuous depending on the domain, the default score averages 3 attempts, and $/task is published. It is a true frontier benchmark - the best model scores 19%.

**Overall board, read Nov 26 2026** (26 models; score, $/task): **GPT-6.2 Astra 19.3% ($46.76)**, **Claude Fable 5.2 16.4% ($88.15)**, **Claude Opus 5.5 11.9% ($51.87)**, GPT-6.2 Sol 11.5% ($9.22), Gemini 4 Carbon 10.9% ($16.29), **Gemini 4 Neon 10.1% ($29.04)**, GPT-6 Astra 9.6% ($54.53), Muse Wonder Full 9.0% (no price), Claude Fable 5.1 8.5% ($67.16), GPT-6.1 Terra 8.5% ($15.02), GPT-6.1 Sol 8.4% ($9.80), Claude Sonnet 5.5 8.2% ($45.27), Qwen 4 Max 7.4% ($11.72), Gemini 4 Argon 7.3% ($12.79), Muse Wonder 6.8% ($15.63), Kimi K3.1 Pro 6.3% ($19.83), DeepSeek 4.1 Flash 5.4% ($0.94), GPT-6 Luna 5.1% ($0.92), Claude Haiku 5.5 4.9% ($3.98), Grok 4.7 3.9% ($16.34), GLM 5.3 2.8%. ## Beyond intelligence - false-premise detection (BullshitBench)

A capability AA, Arena, and GDPx miss: **does the model call out a nonsensical or false premise instead of building on it?** On [BullshitBench](https://petergpt.github.io/bullshit-benchmark/) (v2: 100 nonsense prompts, 13 techniques, 5 domains, models run with no system prompt; clear-pushback rate over **all attempts** from `leaderboard.csv`, repo pulled 2026-09-23 - the dashboard's headline instead **excludes refusals** from the denominator, so heavy refusers such as Fable 5 and 5.1 read ~10 points higher there): **Claude Opus 4.8 is still #1 at ~95%**; the Claude 5 generation is lower - **Sonnet 5 ~80%, Fable 5.1 64-77%, Opus 5 70-73%**; **GPT-6 Astra jumped to 64-69%** (GPT-5.6 Sol ~47%, Terra ~47-54%, Luna ~37-41%); **Gemini 3.7 Flash ~31-35%**. The durable lessons hold: **raising effort doesn't help and often hurts** (Fable 5.1: 77% at `low` vs 64% at `max`), and **refusal ≠ pushback** (Fable 5 and 5.1 decline 11-36% of prompts instead of naming the flaw; Fable 5.2 declines 2%). **v2 additions through Oct 13 (clear-pushback rate):** Opus 5.5 62-63%, Sonnet 5.5 58-60%, **Haiku 5.5 53-57%** (Haiku 4.5: 77%), GPT-6.1 Sol 59-65%, GPT-6 Sol 54-59%, GPT-6 Luna 58-59%, **Gemini 4 Argon 65-66%** (but 13-15% outright acceptance vs ~4% for Opus 5.5 / GPT-6 Astra), Kimi K3 72-74% (as the CSV reads on Nov 26; this file earlier quoted 44-58%), Grok 4.6 56-66% - and again **higher effort scored lower** for Argon and Grok 4.3/4.6. The whole Claude 5.5 family pushes back less than Opus 4.8 / Haiku 4.5 did. **v2 additions on Nov 18 (clear-pushback rate at the lowest and highest effort run):** **Gemini 4 Hydrogen 62% at `low` and 90% at `xhigh`** (the best current model among the vendors this skill covers; Qwen 4 Max also reaches 90% at `max`), **Gemini 4 Neon 67% and 84%** (added by Nov 26), Grok 4.7 75% and 72%, Gemini 4 Carbon 61% and 72%, Claude Fable 5.2 62% and 66% (2% refusals), Kimi K3.1 Pro 50% and 60%, **GPT-6.2 Astra 49% and 55%** (GPT-6 Astra: 64-69%), GPT-6.1 Terra 54% and 53%, **GPT-6.2 Sol 46% and 42%** (6.1 Sol: 59-65%; it accepted the nonsense outright on 17-25% of prompts). So both GPT-6.2 models push back less than the models they replace, and in this batch higher effort scored higher for six of the nine models (Hydrogen by 28 points, Neon by 17) - the effort rule is a default to test, not a law. Not yet benchmarked: Gemini 3.8 Flash. If your app must not act on broken user premises, weight this - and fix it with explicit prompting + model choice, not the effort knob.

## Picking a model - decision rules

- **Highest ceiling** → **Claude Fable 5.2** (AA v5 #1 and first on the Agent arena; its default `high` scores 61.9 for $9.08) when 30-day retention is acceptable (or you have Anthropic's ZDR authorization, or qualify for AWS's ZDR offer on Bedrock) and turns can run long - Anthropic still recommends starting on Opus 5.5 and moving up when your evals at higher effort fall short; **GPT-6.2 Astra** (AA v5 #2: 62.2 at `max` for $4.00, 60.3 at `high` for $2.07; first on GDPx) when you want near-top scores at a fraction of the cost per task and generation time, OpenAI's stack, computer use, or template-faithful documents - add the blocker-disclosure and premise-check lines from `gpt.md`, and expect refusals on security and biology work; **Gemini 4 Neon** (AA v5 #3: 61.5 at `xhigh` for $6.44, 59.8 at `high` for $3.74; #3 on the Agent arena) when you want Google's stack, or a top-three score from a model that mostly abstains instead of guessing (14% hallucination against 54-60% for the two above); **Claude Opus 5.5** (AA v5 #5 at `max`, #2 on the Agent arena, #3 on GDPx, $4/$20, ZDR) as the default top pick on Claude and the strongest Claude under standard ZDR; **Gemini 4 Carbon** (AA v5 #4) when the work needs the deepest thinking per turn, the lowest hallucination rate (4% at `medium`) or long unattended runs - on AA Neon `xhigh` now scores more for less and Neon `high` comes within 0.3 points for half the cost, and Carbon's price doubles to $4 / $20 on Dec 1; **Kimi K3.1 Pro** when you want open weights (released Nov 4) at the top of the open-weight band.
- **Claude, Opus 5.5 or Fable 5.2** → price them per task, not per token: on AA v5, Fable 5.2 `low` (53.9, $3.10) beats Opus 5.5 `high` (51.8, $3.46), and Fable 5.2 `medium` (56.3, $3.80) is 1.2 points under Opus 5.5 `xhigh` (57.5, $6.05) for ~37% less. The catches: 30-day retention by default instead of ZDR, a separate rate limit, longer turns, and prompts that need the Fable 5.2 review in `claude-fable.md`.
- **Default agentic coding** → **Opus 5.5** (FrontierCode 2.0 leader on AA's Nov 7 pull; Anthropic reports Fable 5.2 higher), starting at its `medium` default and raising effort for hard tasks (v5 rewards effort: `high` 51.8, `xhigh` 57.5); for the best capability-per-dollar, **GPT-6.2 Sol** (`medium`-`xhigh`: 52.4-55.6 on AA v5 for $0.41-0.65 per task; second on GDPx's software-engineering domain; OpenAI reports it within 0.5 points of 6.2 Astra on FrontierCode 2.0 at `max`); on Google's stack, **Neon** at its default `high` (Google reports 65.2% on Terminal-Bench 4.1 and 46.2% on FrontierCode 2.0, 0.6 and 1.1 points behind Opus 5.5; it scored only 4.4% on GDPx's software-engineering domain, so test it on your own repository work); on Claude, Sonnet 5.5 at `medium` for well-specified tasks (fast) - at `xhigh`+ Opus 5.5 is usually cheaper per task.
- **Everyday business / support / internal tools** → **GPT-6.2 Sol** (`low`/`medium`) or **GPT-6.1 Terra** (`medium`/`high`), or **Sonnet 5.5** (`low`/`medium`) when you need Claude behavior.
- **Fast multimodal / agentic work at scale** → **Gemini 4 Hydrogen** (`medium` or `high`; it replaces 3.8 Flash) when throughput and per-token speed are the constraint, alone or as the execution model under Carbon or Neon; if cost per task is the constraint, GPT-6.2 Sol or GPT-6.1 Terra costs less for the same AA score.
- **High-volume classification / routing / extraction** → **GPT-6 Luna** (or OpenAI's **Decisions API** for typed answers with free output tokens), **Gemini Decision 1** (Google's Decisions API, Nov 11: calibrated yes/no, choice and score answers, zero output tokens, $0.10 per 1M input - `gemini.md`), or **Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite**; on Claude, **Haiku 5.5** at `low`/`medium` (keep prompts under 100K tokens).
- **Mid tier on OpenAI** → **GPT-6.1 Terra** (on the AA cost frontier from 29 to 45 points) for computer use, concise writing, and scoped fixes; GPT-6.2 Sol for harder coding and agentic work.
- **Live X/Twitter data** → Grok 4.7 with server-side `x_search`.
- **Strict instruction-following / adherence** → Claude 4.7+ (literal following) or GPT-6. (Gemini 3.1 Pro led IFBench on AA's old index, which v5 no longer reports; Google itself points Pro-tier work to Neon.)
- **Professional-domain agent work beyond coding** (finance, legal, science, hardware, security) → check the matching GDPx domain board, not the overall score: rankings flip by domain and by whether safety classifiers apply, and finance and legal were close to unsolved when last read (Nov 7).
- **Must not build on false premises** → Opus 4.8 still leads by far; among the current models this skill covers **Gemini 4 Hydrogen at `xhigh` (90%) and Gemini 4 Neon at `xhigh` (84%)** lead, then Grok 4.7 (72-75%), Carbon at `high` (72%), Argon (65-66%) and Fable 5.2 (62-66%); **GPT-6.2 Astra (49-55%) and GPT-6.2 Sol (42-46%) score below the models they replace**, so add an explicit premise-check instruction when you move to them. Don't count on the 5.5-generation Claude models here without one either.
- **Data retention constraints** → Fable 5.x requires 30-day retention by default (unless Anthropic authorizes ZDR; for Fable 5.2, AWS also offers eligible Bedrock customers ZDR for internal use through at least June 30 2027); Opus 5.5 and the GPT-6 models (6.2 Astra and Sol included) are ZDR-eligible; Gemini 4 models follow the Gemini API's tier terms (paid-tier data isn't used to improve Google's products; Carbon and Neon have no free tier); Gemini's Interactions API stores 55 days by default (set `store=false`; new paid `generateContent` projects also store by default since Oct 26); xAI stores Responses 30 days by default (team-wide ZDR disables stateful features); Moonshot offers ZDR to enterprise customers.
- **Before combining models, sweep effort on one.** Anthropic's measurements: a frontier model at *low* effort often beats a cheaper model at its default on cost per solved task, and in every measured case where the work fit one context window and had no cost tail, the lead model alone at lower effort was cheaper than an orchestrator. Two multi-model shapes earn their keep: an **advisor** (cheap executor consults a frontier model on hard decisions - only pays if the executor actually consults, which low effort can suppress) and an **orchestrator** (frontier lead, cheaper workers - pays as insurance against a routine-task cost tail, or when input exceeds one context window).
- **Routing inside an agent loop** → worth it only if the price gap pays for the router. LangChain + NVIDIA Switchyard (Aug 2026): on a 145-task agent suite, escalation routing between a 30B open model and Claude Opus 4.8 sent ~7% of calls to Opus (which still carried 68% of spend), cutting cost 74% for ~6 points of accuracy; the router's judge was 21% of routed spend because it gets no cache benefit. Break-even: **minimum offload share = judge cost ÷ (expensive-model cost − cheap-model cost)** per run - if your two models are close in price this exceeds 100% and routing can't pay (unless the cheap model is self-hosted). Budget for a range, since escalation rates vary run to run; skip routing on latency-critical or short single-turn workloads.
- **Multi-tier pipeline** (the usual pattern): cheap tier for extract-classify-route (Luna / Flash-Lite / Haiku 5.5) → frontier tier for analysis and generation (Opus 5.5 / Sol / Astra / Fable 5.2 / Neon). Google's own recommended split is Carbon or Neon as orchestrator and verifier with Hydrogen workers (its Antigravity Orchestrator switches between Carbon and Hydrogen automatically); Anthropic's is Opus 5.5 or Sonnet 5.5 with Haiku 5.5 subagents. See SKILL.md Section D.

## Cost-per-task ≠ per-token price

Compare **cost per task at your effort setting**, not headline $/1M. Token efficiency now varies a lot: Opus 5.5 finishes tasks in fewer tokens than Opus 5, GPT-6.2 Astra uses ~41K output tokens per AA task at `max` where GPT-6 Astra used ~52K and Fable 5.2 uses ~274K, while Gemini 3.8 Flash deliberately spends *more* tokens (self-verification) - its low per-token price overstates its advantage on long agentic tasks. Cache-read pricing is now a first-order cost on agent loops: 0.025× input on Fable 5.1 / 5.2, 0.05× on Opus 5.5 / Sonnet 5.5 / GPT-6.2 Astra / GPT-6.2 Sol / GPT-6.1 Sol / GPT-6.1 Terra / Gemini 4 Argon, Carbon, Neon and Hydrogen, 0.1× on GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6 Sol, Luna and most others (incl. Haiku 5.5 and Kimi K3.1 Pro), 0.25× on Grok 4.6/4.7 (variants differ - Kimi Highspeed 0.05×, Grok 4.5 0.15×; check the provider file). Long-context price steps change the math too: Haiku 5.5 ×5 above 100K-token prompts, Grok ×2 at ≥200K, GPT-6 ×2 input above 272K. And watch scheduled price changes (Gemini 4 Argon and Carbon double Dec 1 2026; Gemini Flash doubles Jan 1 2027).

## Methodology notes

AA's **Intelligence Index v5** (Oct 27 2026; v5.0.2 since November) is built from nine evaluations in four equally weighted pillars: **Knowledge Work** 25% (AA-Briefcase 15%, AutomationBench-AA 5%, GDP.pdf 5%), **Coding** 25% (FrontierCode 2.0 15%, Terminal-Bench 4.1 10% - v5.0.2 replaced 4.0 with 4.1, "the same 66 tasks, scored with updated verifiers", and regraded FrontierCode), **Science** 25% (Terminal-Bench-Science 15%, a private hard subset of CritPt v2 10%), and **General** 25% (AA-Omniscience v1.1 15% - 10% accuracy, 5% non-hallucination - and HLE-Diamond 10%). 60% of the index now uses private test sets or held-out answers. New in v5: FrontierCode 2.0 and Terminal-Bench-Science; upgraded: HLE-Diamond (replacing Humanity's Last Exam), AA-Omniscience v1.1, CritPt v2; removed: GDPval-AA, SciCode, and AA-LCR. v5 tasks are far more agentic (median output tokens per task rose from 35K to 59K), which is why effort and token appetite move cost per task so much more than on v4.3. Arena's Agent arena publishes an index built from real agent sessions (a "net improvement" percentage until mid-November 2026). GDPx averages pass@1 over 3 attempts per task in native harnesses with up to 8 hours per task. BullshitBench uses a 0/1/2 rubric (accepted nonsense / buried challenge / clear pushback) from a 3-judge panel over 100 prompts × 5 domains. All four are one evaluator's methodology - directional. A model's rank on a public benchmark rarely predicts its rank on *your* task (build your own evals - see `evals.md`).
