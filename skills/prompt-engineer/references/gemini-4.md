## Prompting Gemini 4 Argon

Argon is built to run long and unattended, so Google's guidance shifts the work to **before** the run (agree on the goal) and **into** the run (give it something to check against). Google wrote these tips for Argon and repeats them almost word for word for Carbon; it has published no separate set for Neon or Hydrogen, so they are the shared starting point for Gemini 4. From Google's *5 practical tips for building with Gemini 4 Argon* (AI Studio developer guide, Oct 13 2026) and the Vertex developer's guide:

- **Agree on intent up front.** "Clear alignment upfront on measurable goals and key milestones helps you get to your result most effectively." State the goal and success criteria, then ask Argon to **surface clarifying questions** about edge cases first, and run a **pre-flight check** - have it confirm it has the tools and docs it needs and restate the intent. Then "step back and let Argon run to completion" rather than steering turn by turn; it is trained to pause for confirmation before irreversible steps, so you don't need to prompt that in.
- **Give it a verification loop.** "Argon does its best long-horizon work when it can test its own changes against a real feedback loop." Have it write a reproducible verification script, record a baseline, and test every change against it until the target is met; if there's nothing to measure, ask it to build a baseline benchmark first. This is the external signal Section A says a critic step needs.
- **Prompt with the goal, put conventions in files.** Google's worked example (an 11-hour unattended performance run on the ADK codebase) started from a single goal paragraph ("…back each change with a test that would catch a regression") - Argon spent 70 minutes measuring before editing, and its first-round failures were all **missing maintainer conventions** (it changed a public JSON schema, ran only nearby tests). Round two put those conventions in `.agents/skills` (a short `SKILL.md` index pointing to `references/` files) and stated the deliverable ("Open one pull request … with before and after numbers in its description. Don't merge it.") - the run then used the full test suite. Lesson: the prompt carries the goal and the deliverable; durable rules live in skills/`AGENTS.md`.
- **Orchestrate with a faster model.** Google's recommended topology: **Argon (`medium` or `high`) as lead orchestrator** for architecture, planning, and review; **parallel, well-scoped work to cheaper subagents** - the Argon guide (Oct) names 3.8 Flash; since Nov 12 Google's recommended execution model is Hydrogen (*Prompting Gemini 4 Hydrogen*). An **adversarial critic subagent** run at the end of each round ("list at least 3 specific flaws to fix … keep iterating until the critic rates it 9/10") is Google's pattern for open-ended quality.
- **Ask it to reflect into instructions.** "Having Argon write those lessons into `AGENTS.md` or a reusable skill means future sessions take the direct path on the first try" - e.g. *"Investigate our current session to see where things stalled or required extra steps, and analyze why. Propose updates to our AGENTS.md or custom skills."*
- **Point at a reference instead of describing a look.** For visual work give 1-2 concrete references, or have Argon distill typography, palette, and layout rules into a short `DESIGN.md` first.
- **Style.** Argon "answers questions more directly with less hedging, doesn't repeat itself more than necessary, and skips clichés"; on long tasks it reports progress more often. Prompts that pushed earlier Gemini models to elaborate may now over-correct - test before carrying them over.
- **Request shape (Vertex guide):** no assistant prefill (a history can't end on a `model` turn); **no unstructured text before a tool call** - log intermediate state through a dedicated `update(previous_step, plan, next_step)` function, and separate inline instructions with `\n\n`; **"each FunctionResponse must match the id, name, and order of the preceding FunctionCall"**; and **"prefer sequential tool execution turns in multi-step tool loops."**
- **Thinking level:** `low` for classification, summarization, extraction, and light analysis/writing; **`medium` (default) for most work, including complex code, tool use, and document Q&A**; `high` for multi-file engineering, proofs, architecture, hard math/science, and vulnerability discovery. Google: "Run your evaluations at all three levels, then pick the lowest level that meets your quality bar." On BullshitBench `low` slightly *outscored* `high` - the Section A finding that effort doesn't fix false-premise acceptance holds for Argon. (Carbon and Hydrogen went the other way in the Nov 18 2026 runs: Carbon 61% clear pushback at `low` and 72% at `high`, Hydrogen 62% at `low` and 90% at `xhigh`.)

## Prompting Gemini 4 Carbon

Google's *Gemini 4 Carbon: Developer Guide* (AI Studio, Nov 10 2026) repeats Argon's five tips almost word for word - agree on intent and run a pre-flight check, give it a verification loop (have it build a baseline benchmark if you have none), point at concrete references or a `DESIGN.md` (new with Carbon: Google's `gemini-frontend-design` skill, `npx skills add google-gemini/demolab --skill gemini-frontend-design`, which Google says beats no skill and popular third-party design skills, and the `gemini-api-cli` skill for generating images, video, speech or music from the terminal), run it as the orchestrator, and have it reflect lessons into `AGENTS.md` or a skill - so everything in *Prompting Gemini 4 Argon* above applies. What changes:

- **Pick Carbon for depth, Argon for throughput.** Same API, price and window; switching is a model-string change. Google: Carbon for "complex root-cause debugging, architectural design, dense multimodal synthesis, scientific proofs, and security auditing"; Argon for "interactive coding, day-to-day feature work, and fast production loops." Since Nov 18 Google's model chooser no longer lists Argon and splits that second role: everyday coding and routine edits go to Hydrogen, complex interactive work (multi-file refactoring, repository-wide debugging) to Neon (*Prompting Gemini 4 Neon*).
- **Give it the lead and verifier roles in a multi-agent setup.** Google pins Carbon (`medium` or `high`) "as the lead orchestrator and verifier to break down the architecture, define interfaces, and audit completed changes", with parallel implementation on cheaper models (its guide names 3.8 Flash or Argon; Google's Hydrogen launch pairs Carbon with Hydrogen workers and verifiers - see *Prompting Gemini 4 Hydrogen*).
- **Budget for longer turns.** Keep `thinking_level` at `medium` unless a workload needs `high`, raise `max_output_tokens` to at least 32,768, and allow HTTP timeouts of 15-20 minutes or use background execution.

The Oct 28 launch post's behavior claims that change how you prompt:

- **It surfaces discrepancies before acting.** "Rather than guessing or forcing a path forward, Carbon retrieves the relevant sources and surfaces discrepancies to the user before acting." On ambiguous tasks expect more check-ins than Argon; Argon's up-front alignment (clarifying questions plus a pre-flight check before an unattended run) is how you keep a long run from stopping on them.
- **It reports what it couldn't do.** It "puts methodological caveats directly into the deliverable or withholds blocked actions with a clear list of what remains required, rather than fabricating or hedging around missing evidence." Give it somewhere to put that list (a section of the report, a `blocked` field) and treat an open item as unfinished work, not a failure.
- **It stays inside the scope.** Google reports 26% less overreach and 29% less under-service than Argon, "staying strictly within scope" - so state the scope you want rather than adding anti-scope-creep guardrails, and say so explicitly when you *do* want adjacent fixes.
- **Effort:** `medium` is the default. Google: `low` for "high-speed classification, routing, and targeted extraction", `high` for "multi-file root-cause debugging, security vulnerability patching, formal math, and upfront architecture design". On AA's v5 index `low` was not cheaper per task than `medium` (54.2 at $7.19 vs 58.4 at $6.59, at list price; Nov 26) and scored lower [one evaluator], so sweep all three on your own tasks before assuming `low` saves money.

## Prompting Gemini 4 Neon

Google has published no Neon prompting guide of its own (checked Nov 26 2026): the what's-new page, the Nov 18 launch post, Google Cloud's developer's guide and the model card describe where it fits and how it behaves. The request-shape rules and the working pattern in *Prompting Gemini 4 Argon* (agree on the goal, give it something to verify against, conventions in files) are the starting point; test them on Neon.

- **Where it fits.** Google: Neon "delivers deeper reasoning than Hydrogen while finishing faster with fewer output tokens than Carbon. Choose it for multi-file refactoring, repository-wide debugging, and analytical tasks where a developer or coding agent is waiting on the response." Keep Hydrogen for everyday loops, and "escalate to Carbon when Neon at `high` or `xhigh` thinking gets stuck". On AA's index (Nov 26 2026) Neon `high` scores 59.8 for $3.74 per task in an estimated 884 s; Carbon `high` scores 60.1 for $7.76 in 2,374 s.
- **Default `high`; coming from Carbon or Argon, pin the level you want.** Those default to `medium` and stop at `high`; Neon defaults to `high` and adds `xhigh`, so a request that never set a level thinks harder after the switch. Google Cloud: "If your workload relied on MEDIUM as the default on gemini-4-argon or gemini-3.8-flash, explicitly set thinking_level="MEDIUM" to keep equivalent reasoning latency." Google's table: `low` for cost- and latency-sensitive work ("Still capable on agentic tasks"); `medium` for "Interactive coding, live UI prototyping, responsive chat, and latency-sensitive tool calls"; `high` the "Recommended default for most tasks"; `xhigh` for "hard, multi-step engineering, analytical, and scientific problems where `high` falls short". The last step is expensive: on AA's index `xhigh` adds 1.7 points over `high` for 72% more cost and 85% more time.
- **Use it as the orchestrator over Hydrogen workers.** Google's launch post: Neon runs "standalone or acting as a fast, high-judgment Orchestrator directing parallel Hydrogen subagents"; Google Cloud: "Plan and verify multi-step software engineering or analytical workflows with gemini-4-neon and pass previous_interaction_id to gemini-4-hydrogen subagents". Switching models drops the earlier model's thought signatures (`gemini.md`, *Thought preservation and signatures*).
- **It stays in scope and finishes more of the task than Carbon.** Google reports under-service down 43-56% and overreach down 13-35% against Carbon "across different effort levels", and says Neon is "trained to complete multi-step work without cutting corners, fabricating results, or overstepping user boundaries". As with Carbon, state the scope you want and say so when you do want adjacent fixes; don't add anti-scope-creep guardrails first.
- **It refuses less than Hydrogen.** Model card: unjustified refusals are 3.00 points lower than Hydrogen's ("particularly on multimodal prompts"), and Google says it pairs Carbon-level safeguards against cyber and biosecurity misuse with "declining far less legitimate work in these areas" (it refused 1.98% of Terminal-Bench 4.0 tasks). If you added workarounds for benign refusals on Hydrogen, re-test them on Neon before carrying them over.
- **Long sessions.** Google says Neon "cuts down on redundant file reads, shares progress as it works, and handles context compaction reliably in long sessions". Reasoning tokens at `high` and `xhigh` still pile up when you preserve signatures: Google Cloud says to "avoid overly low context-compaction thresholds in multi-turn coding harnesses, or set thinking_level="MEDIUM" on latency-sensitive tool turns".
- **The harness moves the score a lot.** Google's own SWE-sweep numbers for Neon: 12.8% in the mini-SWE harness and 39.0% in Antigravity. Evaluate Neon inside the harness you will ship, not in a bare loop (`evals.md`).
- **Check facts instead of trusting them.** AA measures a 14% hallucination rate at `high` (Carbon 4-11%, Hydrogen 9%), and on BullshitBench Neon pushed back on 67% of false premises at `low` and 84% at `xhigh`, so on Neon higher effort helps. The model card still lists hallucinations as a known limitation - ground factual answers in retrieved text.

## Prompting Gemini 4 Hydrogen

From Google's Hydrogen pages (the Gemini API what's-new page, the Nov 12 launch post, the Vertex developer's guide, and the model card):

- **Default `high` is the recommended setting** "for most tasks, including complex coding, agentic execution, tool calling, and multimodal knowledge work"; `medium` for interactive coding and latency-sensitive tool turns; `low` for high-throughput work; `xhigh` only where `high` falls short. Google: "Run your evaluations at all four levels with a high `max_output_tokens` so the limit doesn't cut answers short. Then pick the lowest `thinking_level` that meets your quality bar." Coming from 3.8 Flash, note the default moved from `medium` to `high` - set it explicitly if you want 3.8 Flash's cost profile.
- **Declare tools natively.** "Do not prompt the model via system instructions to emit custom raw-text JSON tool calls with zero declared tools, which can return `MALFORMED_FUNCTION_CALL`" - use function calling or structured output.
- **It flags missing context instead of guessing** (Google's explanation of the 5× drop in fabricated claims). Give it a place to report what it couldn't find, and don't add "always produce an answer" pressure that works against this.
- **It refuses benign requests more often than 3.8 Flash.** Google's model card: "unjustified refusals are higher, particularly for multimodal prompts" (+5.57 points on Google's automated test of "benign or borderline prompts that can be safely fulfilled"; refusal tone is about the same). Before moving a 3.8 Flash workload, add benign cases that look sensitive, and image, PDF and video inputs, to the eval set and compare refusal rates. Google gives no fix; the usual one is to state the legitimate task and audience in the system instruction.
- **Use it as the execution model under Carbon, Neon or Argon.** Google: "Have Carbon plan system interfaces or resolve hard roadblocks, then delegate code edits, parallel subagent tasks, and test runs to Hydrogen." You can switch models across turns of one session with `previous_interaction_id`; Google's Antigravity Orchestrator does this automatically and reached "near-Carbon task success rates" on Terminal-Bench 4.0 with 24% less quota and 19% faster. In stateless mode, still resend the earlier model's thought blocks unchanged when you switch: "The backend manages compatibility" (Gemini API thinking guide). Mind the cache and signature effects of switching (thought signatures from another model are dropped).
- **Parallel reads come naturally.** Hydrogen "batches read and search tool calls in parallel"; keep parallel calls in one turn and their responses in the next (`gemini.md`, *Thought preservation and signatures*). Google also says it "better adapts when tool calls fail".
- **Reasoning tokens pile up across turns** at `high` and `xhigh` when you preserve signatures: set `max_output_tokens` high enough for thinking plus answer, and avoid very low compaction thresholds in multi-turn coding harnesses (Vertex guide). The model card adds that it "may use more tokens to maximize performance, especially at higher thinking levels" and lists "occasional slowness or timeout issues" as a known limitation.

## Migration to Gemini 4 Argon or Carbon

From 3.1 Pro Preview or from a Flash model being promoted to the top tier. (Since Nov 18 Google points both at Neon first - *Migration to Gemini 4 Neon* - and at Carbon "for your hardest problems".) Google's `gemini-api-dev` skill offers `migrate my app to Gemini 4 Argon` (or Carbon).
- Model ID `gemini-4-argon` or `gemini-4-carbon` (both stable). Treat it as a new family, not a drop-in: re-run evals at `low`, `medium`, and `high` and keep the lowest level that passes.
- **Raise `max_output_tokens` to ≥ 32,768** (check framework and eval-tool defaults too); lower `thinking_level` to cut tokens instead of shrinking the cap. Raise client timeouts to ≥ 15 minutes or use background execution.
- Remove `temperature` / `top_p` / `top_k` (ignored, 400 later), `candidate_count`, `frequency_penalty`, `presence_penalty`, any `thinking_budget`, and `minimal`.
- **Drop assistant prefill** and histories ending on a `model` turn; force shape with `response_schema`.
- **Explicit caches don't carry over** - rely on implicit caching: static prefix first, ≥ 4,096 tokens, and expect weaker hits on small agent-loop turns (the 2,050-token rule in `gemini.md`, *Caching*).
- Function calling: exact id/name/order symmetry on responses; no free text before calls (use an `update()` function); prefer sequential tool turns.
- Remove word-count and length targets from prompts - Gemini 4 models ignore them; trim elaboration nudges written for terser 3.x models and re-check output length.
- Rewrite long agentic prompts around a **measurable goal + a verification script + a stated deliverable**, and move conventions into `AGENTS.md`/skills (see *Prompting Gemini 4 Argon*).
- Budget: $2 / $10 until Nov 30 2026, then **$4 / $20** - compare cost per task against GPT-6.2 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 at the price you'll actually pay, and against Neon ($3 / $15), which Google now recommends for most Argon workloads.
- **Argon → Carbon:** "no request schema changes" - change the model string, keep `thinking_level` at `medium` unless a workload needs `high`, and make room in `max_output_tokens` and HTTP timeouts for longer turns. Keep Argon where speed and token spend matter; re-run evals rather than assuming Carbon is better on your task. See *Prompting Gemini 4 Carbon*.
- **Thought signatures:** follow the Gemini 4 rules in `gemini.md`, *Thought preservation and signatures* (every earlier turn's signatures; first parallel call only; set `prefix_mismatch_behavior="DROP"` if your harness edits or compacts history).

## Migration to Gemini 4 Neon

Google's upgrade target for 3.1 Pro Preview, for "general coding and enterprise reasoning workloads" on Argon, and for complex coding or science workloads on 3.8 Flash (Google Cloud's developer's guide); `gemini-api-dev` offers `migrate my app to Gemini 4 Neon`.
- **From Carbon, Argon or Hydrogen:** "no request schema changes" - change the model ID to `gemini-4-neon`. Check three things: the default thinking level (`high`; Carbon and Argon default to `medium`, so a request that never set `thinking_level` now runs at `high` - pin a level on purpose. Google's advice for Argon is `medium` "to keep equivalent reasoning latency"; coming from Carbon, choose with your evals, because Neon `medium` is not Carbon `medium` - AA: 54.9 against 58.4); the tier (paid only - a free-tier Hydrogen project can't call it); and on Vertex the location (`global`, `us` or `eu` - an Argon deployment in `europe-west2` or `asia-southeast1` has to move).
- **From Argon, non-streaming output doubles:** 65,536 tokens per foreground response, "up from 32,768 non-streaming tokens on gemini-4-argon"; long decode works the same way (`gemini.md`, *Output budget and long decode*).
- **From 3.1 Pro or a Gemini 3 Flash model:** everything in *Migration to Gemini 4 Hydrogen* below applies (one shared window, `thinking_level` only with no `minimal`, no explicit caching, no sampling parameters, native tool declarations, thought signatures, no prefilled model turns, every token billed). 3.1 Pro defaulted to `high` too; `xhigh` is new.
- **Price:** $3 / $15 with no intro period. Against Carbon and Argon that is 50% more per token until Nov 30 and 25% less from Dec 1; against Hydrogen it is double. Compare cost per task, not per token: on AA's index (Nov 26 2026) Neon `high` costs $3.74 per task, Carbon `high` $7.76 at list price, Hydrogen `high` $1.87.
- Re-run evals at all four levels (`low`, `medium`, `high`, `xhigh`) in your own harness, with a `max_output_tokens` high enough that it doesn't cut answers short, and keep the lowest level that passes (Google: "pick the lowest `thinking_level` that meets your quality bar").

## Migration to Gemini 4 Hydrogen (from 3.8 Flash)

Google's recommended path for 3.8 Flash and earlier Gemini 3 / 2.5 workloads; `gemini-api-dev` offers `migrate my app to Gemini 4 Hydrogen`.
- Model ID `gemini-4-hydrogen`; upgrade the Gen AI SDK (Vertex guide: Python `google-genai` ≥ 2.34.0, JS `@google/genai` ≥ 2.32.0, Go ≥ 1.77.0, Java ≥ 1.80.0).
- **Window:** 3.8 Flash had separate 1M input / 64K output budgets; Hydrogen shares one 1,048,576-token window across input, thinking and output, with a 65,536-token default and per-request cap. Remove low hardcoded `max_output_tokens` caps or raise them to 65,536; past 64K use background execution or client-side continuation (`gemini.md`, *Output budget and long decode*).
- **Thinking:** default moves from `medium` to **`high`**, `xhigh` is new, `minimal` is rejected (and `thinking_budget` is a 400 on Vertex - use `thinking_level` only). Test `medium` and `high` on your evals; set the level explicitly.
- **Caching:** no explicit caching - remove `cached_content`; implicit caching applies to prefixes of 4,096+ tokens at 95% off, so put static content first. (On Vertex the minimum drops from 3.8 Flash's 6,144.)
- Remove `temperature`, `top_p`, `top_k` (ignored), and per Vertex's guide `frequency_penalty`, `presence_penalty`, `candidate_count` above 1, and prefilled model turns.
- **Tools:** declare them natively (no prompted raw-JSON tool calls); keep thought signatures per the Gemini 4 rules (`gemini.md`, *Thought preservation and signatures*).
- **Billing:** every token is billed, including thinking and the grounding tool's internal instructions and declarations; Search grounding still bills per query.
- Re-run evals: Hydrogen is a different model, not a faster 3.8 Flash; Google reports far fewer fabricated claims and more flagged gaps, which can change pass rates on tasks that rewarded a confident answer. The model card also reports more unjustified refusals than 3.8 Flash, mostly on multimodal prompts - include benign image, PDF and video cases.
